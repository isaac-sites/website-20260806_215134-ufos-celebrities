## Introduction

A striking UFO video can create the impression that an object is accelerating far beyond the capabilities of known aircraft. However, without a reliable estimate of the object's distance from the camera, it is impossible to convert what appears on screen into a trustworthy measurement of speed or acceleration. The camera records only how quickly an object moves across the image, known as its angular motion. To determine real motion through space, investigators also need the geometry of the observation, especially the object's range. This is why many extraordinary claims about "impossible" UFO performance remain unproven: the most important measurement is often missing. NASA's independent UAP study and the US All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) both identify incomplete geometry and limited sensor data as major reasons why unusual-looking footage cannot, by itself, establish extraordinary flight characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Angular Motion Is Not True Speed

A camera records an object's position as an angle against the background, not its physical location. If a bright point shifts across the frame by ten degrees, the recording shows only that angular change. It does not reveal whether the object travelled a few metres nearby or several kilometres far away.

The relationship is straightforward:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A nearby object moving slowly can sweep rapidly across the camera's field of view.
* A distant object moving very quickly can produce exactly the same apparent motion.

The pixels themselves contain no direct information about range unless additional measurements or reference points are available. ## How Distance Changes Every Calculation

Distance affects almost every quantity people try to estimate from a UFO video.

If an object appears to cross a fixed angle in one second:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* At 100 metres away, it may be travelling only a few tens of metres per second.
* At 1 kilometre, the same angular motion represents roughly ten times the linear speed.
* At 10 kilometres, the estimated speed increases by another factor of ten.

</div>

Because the calculation scales directly with range, even a modest error in estimated distance can produce dramatically different conclusions about velocity.

The same uncertainty also affects acceleration. Claims that an object made an "instantaneous" turn often assume a particular distance. If that assumption changes, the calculated acceleration changes as well. A manoeuvre that appears physically impossible at one estimated range may become entirely ordinary at another.

This uncertainty explains why investigators distinguish between apparent motion on the image and reconstructed motion in three-dimensional space. The latter requires far more information than a single video usually provides.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/Information%20Papers/AARO_Effect_of_Forced_Perspective_and_Parallax_View_on_UAP_Observations_2024.pdf?ver=WTwJtQaCvC3BkRlBJCGLzg%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations...</span></span></span>

## Why Moving Cameras Create the Illusion of Extreme Speed

Many well-known UAP recordings were captured from aircraft travelling hundreds of kilometres per hour. In these situations, the observer is moving as well as the object.

This introduces **parallax**, the apparent motion caused by a moving viewpoint. Nearby scenery shifts rapidly across the image while distant objects appear to move much more slowly. If the camera continuously tracks the target, the background may seem to rush past, creating the impression that the object itself is racing across the landscape.

AARO explains that parallax and forced perspective can produce significant errors in estimating an object's:

* speed
* direction of travel
* size
* distance

The office also notes that these effects are particularly important when observations rely on a single fast-moving sensor rather than multiple independent measurements. This does not mean every unusual report is explained by parallax, but it does mean apparent speed alone is not reliable evidence of extraordinary performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/Information%20Papers/AARO_Effect_of_Forced_Perspective_and_Parallax_View_on_UAP_Observations_2024.pdf?ver=WTwJtQaCvC3BkRlBJCGLzg%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations...</span></span></span>

## A Concrete Example: The Importance of Geometry

The US Navy's widely discussed "GOFAST" infrared video illustrates why geometry matters.

At first glance, the object appears to skim just above the ocean at tremendous speed. However, researchers analysing the aircraft's own sensor information showed that apparent image motion cannot be interpreted correctly without accounting for the aircraft's velocity, viewing angle and estimated target distance.

Using the numerical data displayed on the recording rather than visual impression alone, investigators demonstrated that much of the apparent rapid movement results from viewing geometry rather than an independently verified extraordinary velocity. ## What Investigators Need to Reconstruct Motion

To estimate a UFO's actual speed with confidence, investigators typically require much more than the video itself. Useful information includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the camera's exact location
* the observer's speed and direction
* camera orientation and field of view
* zoom level and lens characteristics
* accurate timestamps
* an independent estimate of the object's distance
* ideally, observations from multiple sensors or viewpoints

</div>

With two or more viewing positions, [triangulation]({{ 'triangulation/' | relative_url }}) becomes possible, allowing investigators to estimate range directly instead of guessing it. ## Why Missing Distance Does Not Mean the Object Was Ordinary

An important distinction is that uncertainty cuts both ways. An inability to measure speed accurately does not prove the object was a balloon, aircraft or drone. Equally, it does not support claims that it demonstrated technology beyond known engineering.

Instead, missing distance places the observation into a category where reliable physical conclusions cannot yet be drawn. This is consistent with the broader principle that a UFO or UAP designation reflects incomplete identification rather than evidence of alien spacecraft.

For that reason, professional investigations place considerable emphasis on obtaining additional sensor data instead of relying on visual impressions. Better geometry often resolves apparently extraordinary motion into a much more ordinary explanation—or, in some cases, shows that the available evidence simply cannot support any firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>
