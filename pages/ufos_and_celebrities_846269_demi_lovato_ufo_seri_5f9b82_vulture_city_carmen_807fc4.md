## Introduction

One of the most discussed moments in *Unidentified with [Demi Lovato]({{ 'demi-lovato/' | relative_url }})* takes place not during a UFO sighting but inside an allegedly haunted building at Vulture City, Arizona. In the second episode, *The Star People*, Demi Lovato and paranormal investigators interpret responses from ghost-hunting equipment as communication from a female spirit they call "Carmen". The sequence culminates in Lovato singing to the supposed spirit after concluding that it was uncomfortable around men because of past trauma. The scene became widely shared online, but its significance lies less in any demonstrated paranormal evidence than in how ambiguous electronic signals were woven into a compelling narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.peacocktv.com/watch-online/tv/unidentified-with-demi-lovato/7012570413857604112/seasons/1/episodes/the-star-people-episode-2/07d9feff-d34b-382b-babe-fc1b852995cd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: peacocktv.com">[@peacocktv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">peacocktv.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on peacocktv.com.</span></span></span>

The Vulture City investigation illustrates a recurring feature of paranormal television: devices produce simple, open-ended signals, while investigators supply the interpretation. Rather than showing equipment conclusively detecting an entity, the programme presents a series of assumptions that gradually develop into a coherent ghost story.

Episode two takes the team to Vulture City, an abandoned Arizona mining settlement promoted as a location associated with ghosts, UFOs and unexplained experiences. Inside an abandoned building, paranormal investigator Chris Smith uses electronic ghost-hunting equipment while asking questions directed at an unseen presence. At various points, audible responses from the device are treated as meaningful answers.

The exchange develops through several interpretive steps:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Initial device responses are taken to indicate that an unseen presence is present.
* The investigators identify that presence as a woman they call "Carmen".
* When some questions receive no apparent response, the lack of activity is interpreted as reluctance rather than absence.
* Smith suggests the entity may dislike the men in the room.
* After the men temporarily leave, further beeps are interpreted as confirming that explanation.
* Lovato empathises with the supposed spirit, discussing shared experiences of trauma before singing part of *Skyscraper*.

</div>

By the end of the sequence, what began as isolated electronic signals has become a detailed narrative involving personality, emotional history and interpersonal preferences. None of those characteristics are directly measured by the equipment itself; they emerge through conversation and interpretation. ## What paranormal devices claim to detect

The Carmen sequence relies on equipment commonly used in paranormal investigations, particularly electromagnetic field (EMF) meters or similar electronic detectors.

Manufacturers and paranormal investigators often describe these devices as tools that can register changes in electromagnetic fields or disturbances that they believe may accompany spirit activity. In television investigations, an audible tone or flashing light is frequently treated as a "yes" response during questioning.

From a scientific perspective, however, these instruments do not detect ghosts. EMF meters are designed to measure electromagnetic fields produced by electrical wiring, appliances, radios, mobile phones and many other ordinary environmental sources. Changes in readings demonstrate only that electromagnetic conditions have changed—not why they changed.

The Carmen investigation illustrates this distinction. Viewers observe the equipment producing responses, but the programme offers no independent method of establishing that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* a conscious entity caused the readings;
* the responses corresponded to the questions being asked;
* alternative environmental explanations were excluded; or
* the same results could be reproduced under controlled conditions.

</div>

Consequently, the equipment provides observable events—the lights or sounds—but not direct evidence that those events originated from a ghost.

## Why ambiguous signals become persuasive stories

The effectiveness of the Carmen scene depends less on technology than on [storytelling]({{ 'storytelling/' | relative_url }}).

The device produces only limited information: a tone, light or brief activation. Human participants then transform that limited information into an increasingly detailed account through interpretation.

Several psychological mechanisms make this process compelling:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* **Pattern recognition.** People naturally search for meaningful relationships between events, especially when questions and responses occur close together.
* **Confirmation bias.** Once investigators begin to suspect a particular explanation, later signals are more readily interpreted as supporting that interpretation.
* **Narrative completion.** Audiences generally find complete stories more satisfying than unexplained observations, encouraging investigators to fill informational gaps.
* **Emotional engagement.** Lovato's discussion of trauma creates empathy that shifts attention away from evaluating the device itself and towards the human story being told.

</div>

Importantly, none of these mechanisms requires deception. Participants may sincerely believe their interpretations while reaching conclusions that extend beyond what the equipment objectively demonstrates.

## Did the ghost device really answer Lovato?

Based on what is shown in the episode, the answer is no in any demonstrable sense.

The programme records electronic responses occurring during questioning, but it does not establish that the equipment is communicating with an intelligent entity. The conclusion that "Carmen" answered particular questions depends entirely on investigators assigning meaning to ambiguous signals.

The episode therefore presents several layers of inference:

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}

      });

      });

      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
