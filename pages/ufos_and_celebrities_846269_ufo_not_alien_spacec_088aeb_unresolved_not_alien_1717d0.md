## Introduction

An unresolved UFO case is not evidence that an alien spacecraft was observed. It simply means investigators could not reach a reliable conclusion using the information available. In scientific and investigative work, "unexplained" describes the current state of the evidence, not the nature of the phenomenon itself. A report may remain open because the object was seen too briefly, key sensor data are missing, [witness accounts]({{ 'witness-accounts/' | relative_url }}) conflict, or there is not enough contextual information to distinguish between several plausible explanations. NASA and the US government's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) have both emphasised that limited, poor-quality or incomplete data are the primary reason many cases remain unresolved, and that uncertainty should not be mistaken for proof of an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## How missing data blocks identification

Identification depends on context as much as appearance. A bright point of light in isolation provides remarkably little information.

Investigators typically need details such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* the observer's exact location and viewing angle;
* precise time and weather conditions;
* calibrated camera characteristics;
* radar or infrared measurements;
* nearby aircraft, satellite and launch records;
* multiple independent observations.

</div>

If several of these pieces are absent, multiple explanations remain plausible simultaneously. For example, an object might be a distant aircraft, balloon, drone, astronomical object or optical effect. Without enough measurements, investigators cannot responsibly choose between them.

NASA highlighted precisely this problem in its independent report. Many UAP observations originate from sensors that were never designed to characterise unusual aerial objects, while essential metadata—such as calibration, exposure settings and sensor performance—are frequently unavailable. As a result, even photographs or videos may be inadequate for scientific identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## Why uncertainty is not positive evidence

One of the most common logical errors in UFO discussions is treating a lack of explanation as evidence for a preferred explanation.

This reflects a broader principle of reasoning: absence of an identified cause is not evidence for any particular alternative cause.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Investigators cannot identify an object.
* Therefore, the object remains unidentified.
* It does **not** follow that the object is extraterrestrial.

</div>

Many different explanations may remain possible simultaneously. Without evidence favouring one over the others, none gains support merely because the case is unresolved.

Scientific investigation works by accumulating positive evidence for a hypothesis. Alien spacecraft would require evidence that uniquely supports that conclusion—for example, reproducible measurements demonstrating technology inconsistent with known human engineering or natural phenomena. Simply lacking enough information to identify an object does not meet that standard.

## Official investigations illustrate the difference

Modern government investigations consistently distinguish between unresolved cases and evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

AARO's [public reporting]({{ 'public-reporting/' | relative_url }}) shows that many previously unidentified cases are later resolved as ordinary objects such as balloons, birds, aircraft, satellites or drones once additional evidence becomes available. Other reports remain unresolved because the available information is insufficient for a reliable conclusion. The office explicitly separates unresolved cases from confirmed identifications and has repeatedly stated that it has found no verified evidence that unresolved reports represent extraterrestrial craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/5638be273b753253713a478546849e46" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The report includes many misidentified objects such as balloons, birds, and satellites, though some cases remain unexplained due to insuf...</span></span></span>

NASA reached a similar conclusion. Its independent study found that the current evidence base contains too few high-quality observations to support firm scientific conclusions about the nature of unresolved UAP. Rather than proposing extraordinary explanations, the report recommends improving data collection, calibration and transparency so more reports can eventually be identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## A concrete example of an unresolved case

AARO's public imagery archive includes several reports that remain unresolved. Importantly, the accompanying assessments do not describe extraordinary performance or conclude that alien technology is involved.

In some cases, AARO states that the footage shows a genuine physical object but that its behaviour is unremarkable and additional information would be needed for a more conclusive identification. In another publicly released case, the available mobile phone video was considered insufficient to determine what the object actually was. These reports remain in historical trend analyses rather than being treated as evidence for an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

This illustrates the practical meaning of "unresolved": investigators acknowledge uncertainty rather than claiming certainty where none exists.

## Why unresolved cases can remain unresolved indefinitely

Some reports can never be solved because the missing evidence cannot be recovered.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* witnesses who cannot be re-interviewed;
* deleted or degraded recordings;
* missing radar archives;
* unknown camera settings;
* no secondary observations from other sensors.

</div>

Once those opportunities are lost, later investigators may simply be unable to distinguish between several plausible explanations. Permanently unresolved cases therefore reflect permanent evidence gaps rather than increasing confidence in any extraordinary hypothesis.

## The key distinction to remember

Within the broader discussion of why a UFO does not automatically mean an alien spacecraft, unresolved cases represent a category of uncertainty rather than hidden confirmation.

The critical distinction is straightforward:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Identified cases** have enough evidence for a confident explanation.
* **Unresolved cases** lack enough evidence to determine what was observed.
* **Alien spacecraft** would require independent, affirmative evidence supporting that conclusion.

</div>

An unresolved investigation therefore marks the limits of the available evidence. It does not, by itself, increase the probability that the observation involved extraterrestrial visitors. The most scientifically defensible conclusion is simply that the available information was insufficient to determine what happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA Science...</span></span></span>
