## Introduction

[John Lennon]({{ 'john-lennon/' | relative_url }}) did not keep his reported 23 August 1974 UFO sighting confined to private conversation. Instead, he gradually wove it into his public artistic identity in two distinct ways: first through a handwritten note printed on the artwork of *Walls and Bridges*, and later through the memorable lyric, “There's a UFO over New York and I ain't too surprised,” in *Nobody Told Me*. Those references transformed an intensely personal experience into a recurring element of Lennon's creative world. They also explain why the story has endured in popular culture. The music preserves the fact that Lennon believed he had seen something unusual, but it does not provide independent evidence about what the object actually was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

## The Album Sleeve as Direct Documentation

The most important connection between Lennon's UFO story and his music is not found in a song at all, but in the packaging of *Walls and Bridges*. Released only weeks after the reported sighting, the album includes a handwritten statement:

> “On the 23rd Aug. 1974 at 9 o'clock I saw a U.F.O. – J.L.”

Because this inscription appeared in the original commercial release, it functions as a contemporary record rather than a later recollection. Unlike celebrity stories that emerge decades afterwards through memoirs or interviews, Lennon's claim entered the public record almost immediately after the event. The note was presented without explanation or promotional fanfare, making it feel more like a personal annotation than a marketing device.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

Its placement is also significant. *Walls and Bridges* is widely regarded as one of Lennon's most autobiographical solo albums, reflecting the emotional upheaval of his separation from Yoko Ono during his so-called "Lost Weekend". By placing the UFO statement among the album's artwork rather than issuing it through a press release, Lennon treated the experience as another authentic detail from that turbulent period of his life rather than as a standalone publicity story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

## How the Story Became a Song

The UFO sighting entered Lennon's songwriting more subtly several years later. In *Nobody Told Me*, recorded during the 1980 sessions that ultimately produced *Milk and Honey*, Lennon included the line:

> “There's a UFO over New York and I ain't too surprised.”

The lyric stands among a series of surreal observations describing a confusing modern world:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* everybody is talking but saying nothing;
* everybody is flying yet never touching the sky;
* a UFO appears over New York without provoking much surprise.

</div>

Rather than describing the 1974 sighting directly, Lennon used it as one more example of a world that had become strange enough that extraordinary events no longer seemed impossible. The line therefore functions less as testimony than as a wry personal reference that listeners familiar with the earlier album inscription could recognise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Nobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonNobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity - John Lennon &amp; Yoko OnoJanuary 5, 1984...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1984</span></span></span>

Music historians and Beatles reference works consistently connect the lyric with Lennon's reported 1974 experience, making it one of the clearest examples of a personal anecdote reappearing years later in his songwriting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesbible.com/people/john-lennon/songs/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesbible.com">[Beatles Bible]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesbible.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span></span></span>

## From Personal Memory to Pop Culture

The transition from handwritten [album note]({{ 'album-note/' | relative_url }}) to song lyric greatly expanded the reach of the story.

The *Walls and Bridges* inscription was seen primarily by people who owned the record and examined its packaging. *Nobody Told Me*, by contrast, became an internationally recognised posthumous single following Lennon's death, exposing millions of listeners to the UFO reference even if they knew nothing about the original sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Nobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonNobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity - John Lennon &amp; Yoko OnoJanuary 5, 1984...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1984</span></span></span>

This shift changed the cultural role of the incident. Instead of remaining a curiosity discussed mainly by UFO researchers or dedicated Beatles fans, it became embedded in one of Lennon's best-known late songs. ## Why Songs Preserve Memory but Not Proof

Lennon's musical references are historically valuable because they demonstrate consistency rather than verification.

The album inscription confirms that he publicly claimed the sighting within weeks of the event. The later lyric shows that the experience remained memorable enough to resurface years afterwards during his final period of songwriting. Together, they make it unlikely that the story was invented long after the fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

However, neither the inscription nor the lyric supplies new factual evidence about the object itself. Songs and album artwork record an artist's beliefs, memories and creative choices; they cannot establish the identity of an unidentified aerial object. The enduring cultural legacy of Lennon's UFO story therefore rests on its unusual documentation within his own artistic output rather than on proof of an extraordinary event.

In that sense, the music preserves two separate truths at once: that John Lennon genuinely incorporated his reported UFO experience into his public work, and that the artistic record, however enduring, cannot resolve what he and May Pang actually saw over New York in August 1974.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[johnlennon.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>
