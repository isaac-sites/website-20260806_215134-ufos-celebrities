## Brock's account of the Phoenix Lights

The Phoenix Lights refer to the mass sighting reported across Arizona on the evening of 13 March 1997. Brock has consistently placed himself in Arizona at that time, explaining in interviews that he was travelling by plane to master Modest Mouse's album *The Lonesome Crowded West* when the aircraft entered a holding pattern over Phoenix.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wnyc.org/story/why-it-took-20-years-to-write-modest-mouse-new-single/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wnyc.org">[WNYC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wnyc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">why it took 20 years to write modest mouse new single</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why it Took Isaac Brock 20 Years to Write Modest Mouse&#x27;s New Single &#124; WNYCMarch 3, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why the UFO itself is largely absent from the song

A striking feature of Brock's account is that listeners expecting explicit references to mysterious lights will not find them in "The Best Room", released on *Strangers to Ourselves* in 2015.

Instead, Brock explained that the Phoenix Lights were only "the landing" for the story. After arriving in Arizona, he found himself staying in a run-down hotel where police tape blocked off a large section of the rooms. Those immediate, ordinary surroundings—not the unexplained lights—became the imagery that eventually appeared in the lyrics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wnyc.org/story/why-it-took-20-years-to-write-modest-mouse-new-single/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wnyc.org">[WNYC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wnyc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">why it took 20 years to write modest mouse new single</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why it Took Isaac Brock 20 Years to Write Modest Mouse&#x27;s New Single &#124; WNYCMarch 3, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2015</span></span></span>

## A memory that took nearly twenty years to become music

One reason Brock's connection stands out among celebrity UFO stories is the unusually long creative delay.

According to his own account, the events occurred in 1997 while Modest Mouse were preparing *The Lonesome Crowded West*, yet "The Best Room" did not appear until eighteen years later on *Strangers to Ourselves*. Brock explained that some experiences remain emotionally important without immediately revealing why:

> "Some things are poignant, but maybe the reason they're poignant doesn't become clear for almost two decades."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wnyc.org/story/why-it-took-20-years-to-write-modest-mouse-new-single/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wnyc.org">[WNYC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wnyc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">why it took 20 years to write modest mouse new single</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why it Took Isaac Brock 20 Years to Write Modest Mouse&#x27;s New Single &#124; WNYCMarch 3, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2015</span></span></span>

This distinguishes the song from works intentionally written as UFO narratives. Rather than preserving the appearance of unexplained lights through descriptive lyrics, Brock preserved the emotional atmosphere surrounding the event—travel, uncertainty, isolation and the strange circumstances that followed.

## How this fits the tradition of music preserving UFO stories

Within the broader history of musicians referencing UFO experiences, Brock represents a different model from figures such as [John Lennon]({{ 'john-lennon/' | relative_url }}) or Nina Hagen.

Instead of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* describing a craft,
* arguing for an extraterrestrial explanation,
* or building the song around the sighting itself,

</div>

he embedded the experience beneath an apparently unrelated narrative.

For listeners unaware of the interview history, "The Best Room" functions as an observational song about an unsettling motel and the landscapes of the American West. Only Brock's later explanation reveals that its creative origin lay in one of the best-known UFO incidents in American history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wnyc.org/story/why-it-took-20-years-to-write-modest-mouse-new-single/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wnyc.org">[WNYC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wnyc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">why it took 20 years to write modest mouse new single</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why it Took Isaac Brock 20 Years to Write Modest Mouse&#x27;s New Single &#124; WNYCMarch 3, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2015</span></span></span>

This demonstrates how cultural memory can survive even when the original event disappears from the surface of the artwork. The Phoenix Lights become part of the song's creative genealogy rather than its explicit subject.

## Assessing the evidence

Brock's connection to the Phoenix Lights rests on first-hand interviews rather than on lyrics or [album notes]({{ 'album-note/' | relative_url }}).

Several points are well supported:

* Brock has repeatedly stated that he was flying into Phoenix during the 1997 event.
* He has said the experience inspired the chain of events that ultimately became "The Best Room".
* He deliberately chose not to write a straightforward UFO song, instead focusing on what happened after landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wnyc.org/story/why-it-took-20-years-to-write-modest-mouse-new-single/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wnyc.org">[WNYC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wnyc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">why it took 20 years to write modest mouse new single</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why it Took Isaac Brock 20 Years to Write Modest Mouse&#x27;s New Single &#124; WNYCMarch 3, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 3, 2015</span></span></span>

What cannot be established independently is exactly what Brock saw from the aircraft or whether his observation corresponded to the lights reported by witnesses on the ground. Like many individual recollections connected with the Phoenix Lights, his account is personal rather than evidential. Its historical importance lies less in resolving the mystery than in documenting how a widely discussed UFO incident entered the creative life of a prominent songwriter.

Within the history of music associated with UFO culture, Isaac Brock's contribution is therefore distinctive. Rather than turning the Phoenix Lights into a lyrical spectacle, he transformed an encounter and its aftermath into a song whose origins remained largely hidden until he explained them in later interviews. The result illustrates how UFO stories can be preserved in popular music not only through direct references, but also through the quieter process of artistic memory.
