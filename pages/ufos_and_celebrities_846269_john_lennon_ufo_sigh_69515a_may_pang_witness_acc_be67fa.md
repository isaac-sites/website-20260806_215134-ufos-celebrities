## Introduction

May Pang is the most important corroborating witness in [John Lennon]({{ 'john-lennon/' | relative_url }})’s reported UFO sighting of 23 August 1974 because she said she observed the object independently after Lennon called her onto the terrace of their Manhattan apartment. Her testimony strengthens the historical record by showing that Lennon was not recounting a private experience after the fact. At the same time, her account cannot establish what the object actually was. Much of her most detailed description appeared years later in memoirs and interviews rather than in a contemporary written statement, and some of her estimates of distance, size and altitude rely on human visual judgement under night-time conditions, where such estimates are notoriously unreliable. The result is a witness account that is significant as corroboration of an unidentified observation, but limited as proof of the object's nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[beatlesstory.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

## What Pang said she saw

According to Pang, she was inside the apartment when Lennon urgently called for her to come outside immediately. By the time she reached the terrace, she recalled seeing a large circular object approaching from the direction of the East River. She consistently described it as resembling a flattened cone or disc with a ring of bright white flashing lights around the rim and a single steady red light on top. She also emphasised that it moved silently, distinguishing it from the aircraft regularly approaching nearby airports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.good.is/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: good.is">[good.is]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">good.is</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon had a UFO sighting so vivid, he referenced the phenomenon in a song</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John Lennon had a UFO sighting so vivid, he referenced the phenomenon in a song</span></span></span>

In later interviews and in her memoir *Loving John*, Pang expanded on these details. ## Limits of memory and visual estimation

Despite its importance, Pang's testimony has clear evidential limits.

The most significant is that her fullest narrative appeared long after the event. Human memory is reconstructive rather than photographic, and eyewitness research consistently shows that confidence in a memory does not necessarily correlate with objective accuracy, particularly for unexpected events viewed briefly under poor viewing conditions.

Another limitation concerns estimates of size and distance. Pang's statements that the object appeared comparable in size to a Learjet or close enough to strike with a thrown object are impressions rather than measurements. Without known reference points, angular measurements or independent tracking data, observers cannot reliably determine how large or distant an unfamiliar object is against the night sky. A smaller nearby object can appear similar to a much larger distant one.

Night-time viewing also complicates perception. Bright lights against a dark background reduce the observer's ability to judge shape, speed and distance accurately. ## What her testimony can—and cannot—establish

Pang's account is valuable because it independently supports several historical conclusions.

It strongly supports that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Lennon believed he saw something unusual immediately rather than inventing the story years later.
* There were two eyewitnesses present during the observation.
* Both witnesses independently described the same distinctive pattern of lights and silent movement.
* The event became part of Lennon's documented public record within weeks of its occurrence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[The Beatles Story Museum, Liverpool]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

</div>

However, her testimony does **not** establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object's actual size or altitude;
* whether it was genuinely close to the apartment;
* whether it was an aircraft, atmospheric phenomenon or something else;
* extraterrestrial origin.

</div>

Those conclusions would require physical evidence, multiple independently documented observations with known geometry, instrumental recordings or other corroborating data that do not exist for this case.

## Overall assessment

May Pang remains the most important eyewitness alongside John Lennon because she was present during the reported event and consistently described many of the same unusual features over decades of interviews. Her testimony substantially strengthens the historical credibility of the claim that an unidentified aerial object was observed by both witnesses in August 1974.

At the same time, the reliability of her account has natural limits. Its greatest strength lies in corroborating that a shared visual experience occurred; its greatest weakness is that later recollections cannot overcome the inherent uncertainties of human memory and unaided night-time perception. For that reason, Pang's testimony is best regarded as credible evidence that two people sincerely reported an unexplained observation, rather than evidence that establishes the identity or origin of the object they believed they saw.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/ufos/sightings/the-strange-but-compelling-john-lennon-ufo-sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[ufoinsight.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Insight The Strange But Compelling John Lennon UFO Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Strange But Compelling John Lennon UFO Sighting - UFO Insight...</span></span></span>
