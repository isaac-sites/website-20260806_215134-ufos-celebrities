## Introduction

May Pang's presence on the terrace is important because it confirms that [John Lennon]({{ 'john-lennon/' | relative_url }}) was not the only person who saw an unusual aerial object. However, having two witnesses did not mean they could accurately determine how far away the object was, how large it really was, or how fast it was travelling. Both observers stood together on the same terrace and viewed the object from essentially the same position. That gave them confirmation that they shared the same visual experience, but it did not provide the separate viewing angles needed to calculate distance through [triangulation]({{ 'triangulation/' | relative_url }}). Their descriptions therefore strengthen the historical record of the sighting while leaving its physical characteristics fundamentally unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[The Beatles Story Museum, Liverpool]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

## What Triangulation Requires

Triangulation is a geometric method used to estimate the position of an object by comparing simultaneous observations from two or more locations separated by a known distance. Each observer records the direction to the object, and where those sight lines intersect provides an estimate of its location. Once distance is known, apparent size can be converted into actual size, and observed motion can be translated into true speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufobc.ca/yukon/22ufosize.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO*BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO*BCCase 22: Calculation of UFO Size</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO*BCCase 22: Calculation of UFO Size</span></span></span>

The crucial requirement is that the observers occupy meaningfully different positions. A measurable baseline between observers creates slightly different viewing angles, producing the parallax needed for calculation. Professional investigations of aerial phenomena have occasionally been able to estimate distance when witnesses were separated by hundreds of metres or many kilometres, allowing investigators to compare independent lines of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/lockufo53.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[project1947.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PROJECT 1947: A Study of the Lockheed Sightings Dec 16, 1953 - Martin Shough...</span></span></span>

## The Size-Distance Problem From One Terrace

Without an independently measured distance, every estimate of the object's size depended on assumptions.

Pang later recalled that the object seemed close enough that, in her words, it felt as though a well-thrown object might have reached it. Lennon similarly described it as appearing relatively near. These statements describe perceived proximity rather than measured distance. Human vision alone cannot determine whether an unfamiliar object is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* small and nearby,
* medium-sized at an intermediate distance, or
* very large but much farther away.

</div>

Each possibility can produce a remarkably similar image on the retina because observers primarily perceive angular size—the angle an object occupies in the field of view—rather than its true dimensions. Investigators of unusual aerial sightings routinely distinguish between angular appearance and physical size for precisely this reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case52.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 52: Traffic Investigator Photographs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 52: Traffic Investigator PhotographsAugust 3, 1965...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 3, 1965</span></span></span>

The problem becomes even greater at night. Darkness removes many familiar visual references such as surface detail, texture, shadows and atmospheric perspective. Bright lights against a dark sky can also distort an observer's impression of both size and distance.

## Why Perceived Closeness Is Not a Measurement

People naturally estimate distance using a collection of visual cues rather than a single measurement. These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* comparison with familiar objects such as buildings or trees,
* overlapping foreground and background objects,
* visible surface detail,
* changes in focus,
* stereo vision from the separation between the eyes, and
* motion relative to nearby landmarks.

</div>

Several of these cues become weak or disappear entirely when viewing a brightly lit object in the night sky.

Even if Pang and Lennon believed the object was only a few storeys above nearby rooftops, that impression could not establish its actual range. Unless the object could be shown to pass in front of or behind a known structure, or unless its position could be measured independently, the observation remained a visual estimate rather than a geometric measurement. Studies of reported UFO encounters have long recognised that witness estimates of distance become increasingly uncertain when reliable reference points are absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nicap.org/papers/effectsposition.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases...</span></span></span>

## Why Two Witnesses Improve Reliability but Not Geometry

Having two witnesses is still valuable. Pang's account broadly matched Lennon's description of the object's shape, lighting pattern, apparent silence and slow movement, making it less likely that the event resulted from an entirely private perception or later invention. Their agreement strengthens confidence that both observed the same phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[The Beatles Story Museum, Liverpool]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

What it does not do is solve the geometric problem. Because both observers shared essentially the same line of sight, they also shared the same uncertainty. If the object appeared close to Lennon, it would appear similarly close to Pang. Their agreement confirms a common perception but cannot independently determine altitude, distance or physical dimensions.

This distinction is important in evaluating the case. [Multiple witnesses]({{ 'multiple-witnesses/' | relative_url }}) can increase confidence that an event occurred, yet still leave key physical questions unanswered if they all observed it from nearly the same location.

## The Limits This Places on Later Interpretations

Many later discussions of Lennon's UFO sighting attempt to infer how large, fast or extraordinary the object must have been. Those conclusions depend on assigning a specific distance to the object.

Because no reliable distance measurement exists, none of those quantities can be calculated with confidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **True size** cannot be derived from apparent size alone.
* **Altitude** cannot be determined from visual impression alone.
* **Speed** cannot be calculated because the distance travelled is unknown.
* **Acceleration** likewise remains indeterminate.

</div>

Consequently, Pang's testimony is strongest as corroboration that Lennon was not the sole observer, rather than as evidence that resolves the object's physical characteristics. The absence of separated viewpoints means the sighting remains constrained by one of the most common limitations in visual reports of unusual aerial phenomena: without triangulation, appearance alone cannot reveal true scale or distance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufobc.ca/yukon/22ufosize.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO*BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO*BCCase 22: Calculation of UFO Size</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO*BCCase 22: Calculation of UFO Size</span></span></span>
