## Introduction

One of the most misunderstood features of military illumination flares is their apparent ability to remain fixed in the sky. In reality, parachute flares do not hover. They descend continuously under a canopy designed to slow their fall, but at long range their downward movement can become so small in a viewer's field of view that it is difficult to detect. This simple combination of slow descent, great distance and poor night-time visual references explains why rows of military flares have repeatedly been mistaken for stationary aerial objects, including during the later phase of the 1997 [Phoenix Lights]({{ 'phoenix-lights/' | relative_url }}) event. ## How parachutes slow a flare's descent

Military illumination flares are designed to stay in the air for several minutes rather than falling rapidly. After release from an aircraft, the flare deploys a parachute that dramatically increases drag. Instead of dropping like a free-falling object, it descends slowly while a bright pyrotechnic composition burns beneath the canopy.

Technical military manuals describe aircraft parachute flares producing around two million candlepower of light for approximately three to three-and-a-half minutes, with the parachute specifically intended to support a prolonged, controlled descent. The purpose is practical rather than deceptive: keeping a powerful light source over an area long enough to illuminate the ground during military operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.parachutemanuals.tpub.com/TM-9-1370-201-12/TM-9-1370-201-120006.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: parachutemanuals.tpub.com">[Parachute Manuals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">parachutemanuals.tpub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Parachute Manuals Table 1-1. Identification and Method of Launching Flares</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parachute Manuals Table 1-1. Identification and Method of Launching Flares</span></span></span>

Because the flare is descending at a relatively low vertical speed compared with its enormous viewing distance, observers often perceive only its brightness rather than its motion.

## Why distance hides vertical movement

The key reason a descending flare can appear motionless is angular motion—the amount an object appears to move across a viewer's field of vision.

A flare may descend hundreds of metres during its burn, but if it is 80 to 110 kilometres away, that movement occupies only a tiny angle in the sky. Human depth perception is also extremely limited at such distances, particularly at night, making it difficult to judge whether a bright point is moving towards, away from or below the observer.

Several factors reinforce the illusion:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Slow apparent movement.** The parachute reduces the descent rate enough that the change in position over a few seconds is barely noticeable.
* **Single bright light source.** The flare's intense brightness overwhelms subtle visual cues that might reveal its motion.
* **Long viewing distance.** Small vertical changes translate into extremely small apparent movement.
* **Night-time viewing.** Without nearby reference objects, the eye struggles to estimate speed or distance.

</div>

As a result, observers naturally interpret the light as stationary, even though it is descending continuously. This is a limitation of visual perception rather than evidence that the object is genuinely hovering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.parachutemanuals.tpub.com/TM-9-1370-201-12/TM-9-1370-201-120006.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: parachutemanuals.tpub.com">[tpub.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">parachutemanuals.tpub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Parachute Manuals Table 1-1. ## How a dark horizon removes motion cues

The illusion becomes even stronger when the horizon itself is difficult to see.

During the later Phoenix Lights display, the flares were viewed beyond the Sierra Estrella mountains after dark. The mountain ridge was largely invisible, leaving the bright lights apparently suspended against an empty sky. Without a visible landscape beneath them, observers lost the primary reference needed to judge downward movement.

As each flare gradually descended behind the ridge, it disappeared individually. To viewers unaware of the hidden terrain, the lights seemed to extinguish while remaining stationary. When investigators later aligned the night-time recordings with daylight views from the same locations, the disappearance points closely matched the mountain skyline, supporting the interpretation that the flares were simply passing behind the terrain rather than switching off in mid-air.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-i-dont-believe-in-ufos-the-phoenix-lights-19314" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovermagazine.com">[Discover Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovermagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discover MagazineWhy I don’t “believe” in UFOs: the Phoenix Lights &#124; Discover MagazineMarch 7, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 7, 2007</span></span></span>

## Why the illusion feels convincing

The impression of hovering is strengthened by several characteristics of human vision.

The brain expects nearby objects to show obvious motion. When a bright light changes position only slightly over many seconds, it is often classified as stationary. In addition, the eye tends to lock onto bright point sources, making slow drift difficult to perceive without comparing the light to fixed landmarks.

Video recordings can exaggerate this effect. Consumer cameras compress distant lights into small, saturated points with little surrounding detail. Minor movement that would be measurable frame by frame may be almost impossible to notice during normal playback, especially if the camera itself is handheld.

Wind can also alter the spacing between individual parachute flares. ## Why this matters in the Phoenix Lights debate

The apparent lack of movement has often been cited as evidence that the later Phoenix Lights could not have been military flares. However, the hovering appearance is precisely what would be expected from bright parachute-supported illumination flares viewed from many kilometres away over a dark horizon.

This mechanism does not settle every aspect of the wider Phoenix Lights case. It primarily explains why the later row of lights recorded near 10 p.m. could appear stationary despite descending continuously. Earlier reports of moving V-shaped formations involve different observations and require separate evaluation.         .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
