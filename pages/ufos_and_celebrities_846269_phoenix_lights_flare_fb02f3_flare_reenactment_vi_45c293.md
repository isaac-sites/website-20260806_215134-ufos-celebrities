## Introduction

Later recordings of known military flare drops are among the strongest pieces of comparative evidence in the debate over the [Phoenix Lights]({{ 'phoenix-lights/' | relative_url }}). They do not prove that every report from 13 March 1997 involved military flares, but they do demonstrate that the most widely circulated video footage from around 10 p.m. shares distinctive visual characteristics with documented illumination-flare releases. The value of these recordings lies not in simple visual resemblance, but in the fact that they provide a controlled comparison: the aircraft, the type of flare, the training area and the viewing geometry are known. That allows investigators to test whether specific features of the original footage are expected consequences of distant flares rather than unique characteristics of an unidentified object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[Phoenix New Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

## Similarities Between Later Flare Drops and the Original Video

The later recordings reproduced several features that had previously been presented as mysterious.

First, the lights appeared as a loose row rather than a rigid geometric formation. Individual flares drifted slightly relative to one another because each descended beneath its own parachute. This matched the appearance of the later Phoenix footage more closely than the idea of a single solid craft with fixed lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[Phoenix New Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

Second, the lights remained visible for several minutes without rapid movement across the sky. Illumination flares are specifically designed to burn for an extended period while descending slowly, so an apparently stationary display is expected when viewed from many kilometres away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[Phoenix New Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

Third, the lights disappeared individually rather than simultaneously. In the original recordings, this behaviour was often interpreted as evidence that separate lights were switching off. The later [flare videos]({{ 'flare-videos/' | relative_url }}) showed that sequential disappearances naturally occur when independently descending flares pass behind uneven mountain ridges from the observer's viewpoint.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[Phoenix New Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

The importance of these similarities is cumulative. Any single feature might have multiple explanations, but reproducing several unusual visual characteristics under known conditions strengthens the flare interpretation for the recorded 10 p.m. event.

## What Controlled Comparisons Can Establish

The reenactment footage is valuable because it compares an unknown recording with events whose causes are already established.

Investigators were able to compare:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* viewing direction from the Phoenix metropolitan area towards the Barry M. Goldwater Range;
* relative spacing of the lights;
* apparent lack of rapid motion;
* rate of descent;
* timing of individual disappearances; and
* relationship between the lights and the Sierra Estrella ridgeline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[Phoenix New Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

</div>

One of the most influential demonstrations came from image-processing work commissioned for a television documentary. Analysts aligned a witness's night-time recording with a daylight image taken from the same location. Once the invisible night horizon was restored, each light disappeared at the point where it intersected the mountain ridge rather than while still clearly above it. This finding supported the interpretation that the lights were descending behind terrain instead of extinguishing while suspended over Phoenix.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2015/03/alien-lights-at-phoenix-stephenville-and-elsewhere-a-postmortem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on skepticalinquirer.org.</span></span></span>

This type of comparison is considerably stronger than arguing from subjective appearance alone. It relies on measurable geometry rather than eyewitness impressions or expectations.

## Where Visual Resemblance Reaches Its Limits

Although the reenactment videos are persuasive regarding the later footage, they do not resolve every aspect of the wider Phoenix Lights case.

Visual similarity can establish that military flares are capable of producing images closely resembling the famous recordings. It cannot, by itself, identify the exact source of every individual light seen on a different occasion. Multiple phenomena can sometimes appear similar in low-light video, especially when distance, atmospheric conditions and camera limitations are poorly constrained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2015/03/alien-lights-at-phoenix-stephenville-and-elsewhere-a-postmortem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on skepticalinquirer.org.</span></span></span>

More importantly, the comparison applies only where the observational circumstances match. The later recordings address the stationary lights seen around 10 p.m. over the south-west horizon. They do not automatically explain earlier reports of a large moving V-shaped formation observed by witnesses across different parts of Arizona. Even investigators who regard the later videos as strong evidence for the [flare explanation]({{ 'evidence-limits-071e7a/' | relative_url }}) generally treat those earlier reports as a separate evidential question requiring independent assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/the-phoenix-lights-are-no-mystery-6661825/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phoenixnewtimes.com">[phoenixnewtimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phoenixnewtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the phoenix lights are no mystery 6661825</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phoenix New TimesThe 1997 &quot;Phoenix Lights&quot; are no mystery &#124; Phoenix New TimesMarch 14, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2014</span></span></span>

For that reason, the reenactment videos should not be interpreted as proving either that every Phoenix Lights report was caused by flares or that the flare explanation is impossible.
