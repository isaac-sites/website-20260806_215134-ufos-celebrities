## Introduction

The 2013 [Puerto Rico]({{ 'puerto-rico/' | relative_url }}) infrared UFO video, recorded by a U.S. Customs and Border Protection aircraft near Rafael Hernández Airport in [Aguadilla]({{ 'aguadilla/' | relative_url }}), is one of the best-known examples of how camera geometry and limited depth perception can create the appearance of extraordinary flight. For years, the footage was widely interpreted as showing a single object racing across the landscape, splitting into two, entering the sea, emerging again, and finally disappearing. More recent analysis by the U.S. Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) reached a different conclusion: the recording is most consistent with two nearby objects moving steadily with the wind while perspective effects and infrared imaging created the illusion of dramatic manoeuvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## What the infrared footage appeared to show

On 26 April 2013, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. Customs and Border Protection aircraft recorded an unidentified target while operating near Aguadilla, Puerto Rico. The publicly circulated footage appeared to show several remarkable behaviours:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A dark object travelling rapidly at low altitude.
* An apparent crossing from land to sea.
* A sudden split into two identical objects.
* One or both objects apparently entering and leaving the water.
* Final disappearance offshore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/944204/puerto-rico-objects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - Puerto Rico Objects...</span></span></span>

</div>

Taken at face value, these apparent actions suggested what UFO researchers often call a "transmedium" vehicle—one capable of operating seamlessly through air and water. Because infrared imagery records temperature differences rather than visible light, the video also lacked familiar visual cues such as colour, surface texture and horizon detail, making the object's behaviour even harder to interpret.

The apparent sequence became one of the most frequently discussed military-related UFO videos outside the better-known U.S. Navy recordings.

## How flight paths and sensor angles changed the picture

[AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})'s reconstruction approached the incident differently. Rather than relying only on the appearance of the infrared recording, investigators reconstructed the observing aircraft's own flight path, the sensor's changing look angle and local wind conditions. According to AARO, these additional data fundamentally altered the interpretation of what the camera recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

The reconstruction found that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The objects followed a largely straight path instead of executing sudden accelerations.
* Their ground speed closely matched prevailing wind speed.
* The apparent high speed resulted largely from the motion of the observing aircraft combined with the sensor's changing viewing geometry.
* The apparent dive into the ocean occurred because the infrared contrast between the objects and the water temporarily disappeared, not because the objects actually entered the sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

</div>

This illustrates a common problem in interpreting airborne infrared footage. A camera mounted on a fast-moving aircraft continually changes its viewing angle. Without accurate distance measurements, even slow-moving targets can appear to surge sideways or accelerate dramatically against the background.

## Why two objects looked like one transforming craft

Perhaps the most striking feature of the recording is the apparent moment when one object seems to divide into two.

AARO concluded that this impression is better explained by two nearby objects travelling together than by a single object physically splitting apart. An intelligence community reconstruction indicated that both objects maintained independent but closely parallel paths throughout the event. From the changing viewing angle of the aircraft's infrared sensor, they periodically overlapped in the camera's line of sight before separating visually.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

The illusion depends on several interacting factors:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Compressed depth cues.** Infrared imagery provides little information about the distance between separate objects.
* **Changing observation angle.** As the aircraft turned and the sensor tracked the targets, their apparent separation continually changed.
* **Limited image resolution.** Small targets can merge into a single blurred thermal signature before resolving again as viewing geometry changes.
* **Relative motion.** When both the observing aircraft and the targets are moving, the camera records changing perspective rather than a simple overhead view.

</div>

Together, these effects can make two ordinary objects resemble one object transforming into two.

## Why the object seemed to enter the sea

The apparent water entry became one of the strongest arguments for unusual behaviour before the official reconstruction.

According to AARO, however, the event can be explained by the way forward-looking infrared systems display temperature contrast. When the apparent target and the water beneath it have similar thermal characteristics, the object's infrared signature can fade into the background. As contrast returns, the object appears to re-emerge, even though it remained airborne throughout.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

This is a reminder that infrared cameras do not directly show physical outlines. They display differences in emitted or reflected thermal radiation. When those differences become small, an object can temporarily disappear from the image despite remaining continuously visible in ordinary daylight.

## What remains debated

Although AARO expressed high confidence that the recording does not show anomalous flight behaviour, discussion has not completely ended.

Some independent researchers argue that the official reconstruction gives insufficient weight to other reported information, including radar observations and [witness accounts]({{ 'witness-accounts/' | relative_url }}) associated with the incident. ## Why the Puerto Rico case matters

The Puerto Rico infrared video has become an important case study in UFO investigations because it demonstrates that advanced sensors are not immune to visual misinterpretation. Sophisticated infrared systems can record genuine physical events while still producing misleading impressions when distance, perspective and thermal contrast are not independently established.

Within the broader category of famous UFO reports later linked to ordinary explanations, the Aguadilla recording illustrates an important lesson: remarkable apparent motion in a video is not, by itself, evidence of remarkable object behaviour. Accurate interpretation requires reconstructing the motion of both the target and the observing platform, together with sensor characteristics and environmental conditions. In this case, that broader reconstruction substantially changed what the famous footage appears to show.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>
