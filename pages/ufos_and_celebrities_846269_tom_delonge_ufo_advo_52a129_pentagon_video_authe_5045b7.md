## Introduction

The Pentagon’s authentication of the three widely discussed U.S. Navy UAP videos—commonly known as **FLIR1**, **Gimbal** and **GoFast**—settled one important question while leaving several others unanswered. In April 2020, the Department of Defense confirmed that the videos were genuine military recordings taken by Navy aircraft and formally authorised their public release. It did **not** state that the objects depicted were extraterrestrial spacecraft, revolutionary technology, or even behaving in ways beyond known physics. Instead, the Pentagon explicitly said the aerial phenomena remained **unidentified**, a classification that describes the current state of analysis rather than the origin or nature of the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarStatement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; Rele...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/)

This distinction became especially important within the public campaign surrounding Tom DeLonge's advocacy network. Official authentication gave the videos institutional credibility, but it did not validate every interpretation promoted by supporters or critics. Understanding what the Pentagon confirmed—and what it deliberately did not claim—is essential to interpreting the videos accurately.

## Why authenticity and interpretation are different

[Authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) answers one question: **Is the recording genuine?**

Interpretation asks a very different question: **What does the recording actually show?**

These questions are frequently conflated. A genuine military recording can still depict an object whose identity, distance, speed or behaviour cannot be confidently determined from the available data alone.

In intelligence and aviation practice, "unidentified" simply means investigators have not reached a confident identification using the available evidence. It is not shorthand for "alien," "advanced civilisation," or "technology beyond human capability." Numerous possibilities—including ordinary aircraft, balloons, drones, atmospheric effects, [sensor artefacts]({{ 'sensor-artefacts/' | relative_url }}) or incomplete information—may remain under consideration until sufficient evidence allows one explanation to be favoured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarStatement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; Rele...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/)

Consequently, Pentagon authentication should not be interpreted as official endorsement of any extraordinary hypothesis.

## Camera geometry, range uncertainty and missing context

A major limitation of the videos is that they are short infrared targeting-camera clips rather than complete records of each encounter.

Several factors complicate interpretation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Unknown range:** Without independently verified distance measurements, apparent motion can be misleading.
* **Viewing geometry:** Relative motion between the aircraft, target and camera can exaggerate apparent speed.
* **Infrared imaging:** Thermal sensors display heat signatures rather than ordinary visual appearance.
* **Incomplete sensor data:** Radar tracks, pilot displays, telemetry and classified mission information are only partially available publicly.

</div>

These limitations mean that dramatic-looking footage does not necessarily correspond to equally dramatic flight behaviour.

The **GoFast** video illustrates this problem particularly well. Later analysis by the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) concluded that the object's apparent rapid movement close to the ocean was largely a consequence of viewing geometry and parallax rather than extraordinary speed. Importantly, AARO did **not** identify the object itself; it explained that the available evidence no longer supported the widespread assumption that it was travelling at remarkable velocity just above the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/pentagon-navy-uap-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

Similarly, the apparent rotation visible in the **Gimbal** video has generated competing technical explanations. Some researchers argue that it reflects genuine object motion, while others attribute much of the apparent rotation to characteristics of the infrared targeting pod and optical system. ## What the videos did not establish

The Pentagon's authentication did **not** demonstrate that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the objects originated outside Earth;
* they used propulsion beyond known science;
* they accelerated in ways impossible for conventional aircraft;
* they represented secret U.S. programmes;
* they belonged to foreign adversaries; or
* eyewitness interpretations were automatically confirmed by the infrared footage alone.

</div>

Each of those propositions requires additional evidence beyond authentic video recordings.

The videos are only one element within broader incidents that also involved pilot observations, radar returns and other sensor information. Assessing the complete encounters requires integrating multiple sources of evidence rather than relying solely on the released clips.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/pentagon-navy-uap-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

## Why the distinction mattered for Tom DeLonge's advocacy network

Within Tom DeLonge's wider UFO advocacy effort, official authentication represented a significant communications victory because it removed one longstanding objection: claims that the videos were fabricated or fake.

However, authentication did not validate the broader conclusions sometimes attached to them. DeLonge's network frequently argued that the incidents deserved serious governmental attention, a position that gained credibility as the Pentagon acknowledged the recordings and Congress expanded oversight of UAP investigations. Yet the official government position remained more cautious than many public interpretations.

The distinction proved important because two separate claims were often merged in public debate:

1. **The videos are authentic military recordings.**
2. **The videos prove extraordinary technology or extraterrestrial craft.**

The Pentagon endorsed only the first proposition while explicitly leaving the second unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarStatement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; Rele...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/)

## The enduring lesson

The official release of the [Navy videos]({{ 'navy-videos/' | relative_url }}) marked an important shift in transparency but not a definitive solution to the underlying mystery. Authentic military footage can document an event worthy of investigation without establishing what caused it.

The Pentagon confirmed that the recordings were genuine and that the observed objects had not been identified at the time of release. It did not certify any specific explanation, whether mundane or extraordinary. For that reason, the videos remain valuable evidence in the study of UAP, but they are not, by themselves, proof of aliens, revolutionary aerospace technology or any single competing hypothesis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defens/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarStatement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; Rele...</span></span></span>
