## Introduction

[John Lennon]({{ 'john-lennon/' | relative_url }})’s reported UFO sighting of 23 August 1974 might have remained an obscure anecdote had it been preserved only in interviews or memoirs. Instead, it gained an unusual second life through the lyric of **“Nobody Told Me”**, recorded in 1980 and released posthumously in 1984. While the handwritten note inside *Walls and Bridges* documented the claimed sighting, the later lyric transformed it into a memorable cultural reference. Rather than functioning as evidence for an unexplained event, the song helped ensure that Lennon’s account continued to circulate through radio broadcasts, compilations, streaming services and popular discussion long after the original incident had faded from the news.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

> “On the 23rd Aug. 1974 at 9 o’clock I saw a U.F.O. – J.L.”

That sentence was brief, undramatic and offered no explanation for what he believed he had seen. It nevertheless attached the claim permanently to an official commercial release, making it far more durable than a passing interview or rumour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>

When Lennon later wrote “Nobody Told Me”, he did not retell the experience or describe the sighting in detail. Instead, he compressed it into a single line:

> “There's a UFO over New York and I ain't too surprised.”

This shift was significant. The [album note]({{ 'album-note/' | relative_url }}) documented a specific event. The lyric distilled it into an image that listeners could remember without needing to know the background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Nobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonNobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity - John Lennon &amp; Yoko Ono...</span></span></span>

## Why the lyric proved more durable than the story

Songs spread differently from eyewitness accounts. Every radio play, compilation album or streaming playlist exposed new listeners to the line about a UFO over New York, even if they had never encountered the *Walls and Bridges* sleeve note.

Several features helped preserve the association:

* **Its brevity.** The lyric is only one line, making it easy to quote.
* **Its placement.** It appears among a sequence of surreal observations that listeners often remember together.
* **Its repetition.** Unlike the one-off album inscription, the song has been repeatedly reissued, performed and discussed since its 1984 release.
* **Its ambiguity.** Lennon does not explain whether he is recalling a personal experience, commenting on urban mythology or simply adding another strange image to the song's catalogue of absurdities. That openness has encouraged continued discussion rather than settling the question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Nobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonNobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity - John Lennon &amp; Yoko Ono...</span></span></span>

The result is that many people first encounter Lennon's UFO story through the song rather than through contemporary reports of the alleged sighting.

## The lyric's place within the song

"Nobody Told Me" is built around contradictions and observations that make modern life seem simultaneously ordinary and bizarre:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* "Everybody's talking and no one says a word."
* "Everybody's flying and never touch the sky."
* "There's a UFO over New York and I ain't too surprised."

</div>

Within this structure, the UFO is not presented as the song's central subject. Instead, it becomes another example of a world in which unexpected events no longer shock the narrator. The phrase "I ain't too surprised" is especially revealing: Lennon treats the extraordinary as though it belongs naturally alongside the other oddities he lists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/singles/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Nobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonNobody Told Me/O&#x27; Sanity - John Lennon &amp; Yoko Ono...</span></span></span>

This literary framing differs markedly from the 1974 sleeve note, which simply recorded an occurrence without interpretation.

## A posthumous release changed the story's reach

Lennon recorded "Nobody Told Me" during the 1980 sessions that also produced *Double Fantasy*. Following his death in December 1980, the unfinished material was completed and issued on *Milk and Honey* in 1984, with "Nobody Told Me" released as the lead single. It became an international hit, reaching the Top 10 in both the United States and the United Kingdom.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesbible.com/people/john-lennon/songs/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesbible.com">[Beatles Bible]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesbible.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span></span></span>

Because the song emerged after Lennon's death, the lyric acquired a life independent of any later explanation he might have offered. Listeners could not ask him whether the line referred directly to his 1974 experience or whether it had evolved into a broader metaphor. That absence of clarification encouraged decades of interpretation.

The commercial success of the single also meant that far more people heard the lyric than had ever owned an original copy of *Walls and Bridges*. In practical terms, the song became the primary vehicle through which Lennon's UFO story entered popular culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesbible.com/people/john-lennon/songs/nobody-told-me/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesbible.com">[Beatles Bible]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesbible.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beatles Bible Nobody Told Me &#124; John Lennon &#124; The Beatles Bible</span></span></span>

## How later accounts reinforced the connection

Subsequent biographies and Beatles reference works routinely connect the lyric to the reported 1974 sighting. May Pang, who was with Lennon during the period known as his "Lost Weekend", has consistently maintained that both of them observed an unusual silent object over New York and that attempts were made to report the incident. Her recollections are frequently cited alongside the lyric as supporting context for why Lennon included the reference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[The Beatles Story Museum, Liverpool]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

Importantly, these later accounts do not provide conclusive evidence identifying what was seen. Instead, they explain why the lyric is widely understood as a deliberate callback rather than a random piece of imagery.

## Cultural memory rather than physical evidence

The enduring importance of "Nobody Told Me" lies less in proving that Lennon witnessed an unidentified object than in showing how popular music preserves personal narratives.

The underlying sighting remains difficult to investigate because there are no surviving photographs that establish what the object was, and no widely accepted official investigation resolved the incident. Yet the song has ensured that the claim itself remains part of Lennon's public legacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.beatlesstory.com/blog/john-lennon-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: beatlesstory.com">[The Beatles Story Museum, Liverpool]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">beatlesstory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Beatles Story Museum, LiverpoolJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

This illustrates a broader pattern in the history of celebrity UFO stories. Physical evidence may remain limited or contested, but once a claim is embedded in a successful piece of music, it can outlast eyewitness testimony. In Lennon's case, the combination of the 1974 *Walls and Bridges* inscription and the memorable lyric in "Nobody Told Me" created two complementary records: one documenting the claimed event, the other embedding it in popular culture for generations of listeners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.johnlennon.com/music/albums/walls-and-bridges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnlennon.com">[John Lennon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnlennon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Lennon Walls And Bridges</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John LennonWalls And Bridges - John Lennon with The Plastic Ono Nuclear Band/Little Big Horns/and the Philharmanic OrchestrangeSeptember...</span></span></span>
