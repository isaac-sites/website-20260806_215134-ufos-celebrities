## Introduction

[To The Stars Academy]({{ 'to-the-stars/' | relative_url }}) of Arts & Science (TTSA), now operating as To The Stars, became one of the most influential and controversial examples of celebrity-led UFO research. Its distinctive feature was not simply that it investigated unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), but that it deliberately combined scientific aspirations, entertainment production, commercial intellectual property and public investment within a single organisation. That structure attracted attention, funding and high-profile personnel, but it also created governance questions about whether the same organisation could objectively investigate extraordinary claims while simultaneously benefiting from keeping those claims commercially compelling. The issue is less whether TTSA made accurate or inaccurate claims in any individual case than whether its organisational incentives aligned with the cautious standards normally expected of scientific inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000149315221010202/partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2021</span></span></span>

## How TTSA combined research, advocacy and entertainment

From its inception in 2017, TTSA described itself as far more than a research organisation. Securities filings presented it as an integrated enterprise spanning entertainment, science and aerospace, with the stated aim of advancing understanding of unexplained phenomena while developing commercially valuable intellectual property. Rather than treating [documentaries]({{ 'documentaries/' | relative_url }}) or books as outreach after research was complete, the organisation embedded media production into its [business model]({{ 'business-model/' | relative_url }}) from the outset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000114420419029517/tv522799_partiiandiii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tv522799 partiiandiii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">tv522799 partiiandiii</span></span></span>

This produced an unusual combination of roles:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* conducting investigations into UAP and advanced technology claims;
* employing former defence and intelligence officials with relevant experience;
* producing television series, books, films and other media based on those investigations;
* selling branded merchandise;
* raising capital from public investors through Regulation A offerings.

</div>

Unlike a conventional scientific institution, TTSA therefore operated simultaneously as investigator, narrator, publisher and commercial beneficiary of its own story. That structure does not automatically invalidate its work, but it creates incentives very different from those governing independent academic or government research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tm2022367d1 partii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 12, 2020</span></span></span>

A particularly revealing example appeared in the company's own description of its entertainment division. Rather than separating fictional [storytelling]({{ 'storytelling/' | relative_url }}) from scientific investigation, filings stated that the company created media "where the line between science and science fiction is often blurred." That language may be appropriate for entertainment marketing, but it complicates expectations of evidential neutrality when the same organisation also presents itself as conducting serious scientific investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tm2022367d1 partii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 12, 2020</span></span></span>

## What the company's filings reveal about commercial incentives

TTSA's filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission are valuable because they describe the organisation's business model in legal documents prepared for prospective investors rather than promotional interviews.

Several recurring themes stand out.

First, entertainment generated the overwhelming majority of revenue. Annual and semi-annual reports explained that the Entertainment Division created books, films, television, music and merchandise, while the Science and Technology Division generated little or no direct income. In practice, commercially successful media subsidised the more speculative research ambitions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920109040/tm2031843d1_1sa.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tm2031843d1 1sa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 28, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 28, 2020</span></span></span>

Second, management repeatedly explained that future projects depended upon raising additional capital. Offering documents linked research priorities to the amount of investment secured, meaning that scientific ambitions were financially dependent on continued investor confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000114420419029517/tv522799_partiiandiii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tv522799 partiiandiii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">tv522799 partiiandiii</span></span></span>

Third, the filings emphasised intellectual property as a commercial asset. The company sought to create, acquire and commercialise content across entertainment, science and technology, presenting discoveries not simply as knowledge but as potentially valuable proprietary material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000149315221010202/partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2021</span></span></span>

Finally, the organisation acknowledged substantial business risks, including dependence on fundraising, forward-looking assumptions and uncertain commercial outcomes. These disclosures are routine in securities filings, but they highlight that investors were funding a speculative enterprise whose success depended partly on sustaining public interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000114420419029517/tv522799_partiiandiii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tv522799 partiiandiii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">tv522799 partiiandiii</span></span></span>

## Why unresolved mystery can remain commercially valuable

The governance challenge arises because scientific research and entertainment reward different outcomes.

A conventional scientific investigation is expected to publish negative results, revise hypotheses and sometimes conclude that an anomaly has an ordinary explanation. Such outcomes can be scientifically valuable while attracting relatively little public attention.

Entertainment economics work differently. Television series, documentaries, books and ongoing franchises often benefit from continuing uncertainty, cliff-hangers and unresolved questions that encourage audiences to return for future developments.

TTSA's structure meant these incentives existed within the same organisation. Even if individual researchers acted in good faith, the broader business benefited from maintaining public engagement with unresolved mysteries. This creates a structural tension:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* definitive mundane explanations can reduce audience excitement;
* unresolved anomalies encourage continued discussion and media interest;
* long-running investigations support future books, documentaries and other intellectual property.

</div>

Importantly, this is an incentive analysis rather than an accusation of deliberate deception. Organisations can face conflicts of interest without fabricating evidence. Governance questions concern how institutional incentives may influence priorities, communication and framing even when participants believe they are acting honestly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000149315221010202/partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2021</span></span></span>

## The UAP videos illustrate the communication challenge

TTSA's involvement in bringing public attention to the Navy's FLIR1, Gimbal and GoFast videos demonstrates both its strengths and its governance tensions.

The organisation helped elevate material that eventually received official acknowledgement from the US Department of Defense regarding its [authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) as military-recorded footage. That contribution significantly increased public and congressional interest in UAP and encouraged greater governmental transparency.

However, the commercial ecosystem surrounding TTSA also rewarded broader interpretations than official statements supported. Confirmation that footage was authentic military video did not establish that it depicted extraterrestrial technology, yet public discussion often blurred those distinctions. The same organisation promoting investigation was also producing documentaries and other media centred on the significance of the cases. This made careful separation between confirmed facts, informed speculation and commercial storytelling more difficult for audiences to maintain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000110465920072815/tm2022367d1_partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tm2022367d1 partii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 12, 2020</span></span></span>

## Why governance matters more than celebrity

The most enduring lesson from TTSA is organisational rather than personal. Celebrity leadership attracted attention, but the more significant issue was the concentration of multiple institutional roles within one entity.

Good research governance normally seeks to separate:

* evidence collection;
* analysis;
* peer scrutiny;
* public communication;
* financial incentives.

TTSA instead integrated many of these functions into a single corporate structure. That integration created efficiencies in fundraising and public engagement but reduced the institutional distance that often helps scientific organisations resist confirmation bias or commercial pressure.

The organisation therefore became an important case study in the governance risks of celebrity-led UFO research. It demonstrated that public visibility, experienced personnel and genuine investigative contributions can coexist with commercial incentives that reward compelling narratives before independent validation has resolved what the evidence ultimately shows.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710274/000149315221010202/partii.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[SEC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">April 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2021</span></span></span>

      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
