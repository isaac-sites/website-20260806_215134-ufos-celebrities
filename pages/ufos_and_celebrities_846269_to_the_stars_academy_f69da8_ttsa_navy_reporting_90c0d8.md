## Introduction

[To the Stars Academy]({{ 'to-the-stars/' | relative_url }}) of Arts & Science (TTSA) did not create the US Navy's formal procedures for reporting unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), nor is there evidence that it directly wrote or implemented Navy policy. Its influence was instead indirect but significant. By publicising Navy encounter videos, providing a platform for former defence officials and military aviators, and consistently framing unexplained encounters as aviation-safety and national security issues rather than as evidence of extraterrestrial visitors, TTSA helped create the public and political environment in which reporting reform became more acceptable. The Navy's 2019 decision to formalise reporting procedures reflected operational concerns inside the service, but TTSA played an important role in bringing those concerns into public view and reducing the stigma surrounding them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/us-navy-ufo-sighting-guidelines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">us navy ufo sighting guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reporting UFO Sightings to the US Navy Just Got Easier &#124; HISTORYApril 25, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 25, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why pilots lacked consistent reporting channels

Before 2019, Navy personnel could report unusual aerial encounters through existing aviation safety and intelligence systems, but there was no widely understood, standardised process specifically designed for recurring reports of unidentified or unauthorised aircraft. Pilots who experienced unusual encounters often described uncertainty over where such reports belonged and concern that discussing them could damage their professional reputation.

## How TTSA reframed the issue for officials

TTSA's most important contribution was not technical but rhetorical and political. From its launch in 2017, the organisation consistently avoided presenting the Navy encounters primarily as proof of alien spacecraft. Instead, figures including Luis Elizondo and Christopher Mellon argued that unidentified objects operating near military aircraft represented an intelligence and aviation-safety problem regardless of their ultimate origin.

This framing differed from many earlier UFO advocacy campaigns. Rather than asking officials to acknowledge extraterrestrial visitors, TTSA argued that professional military observations deserved systematic collection and investigation. That message proved more compatible with defence institutions, Congress and mainstream journalism.

The publication of the [Navy videos]({{ 'navy-videos/' | relative_url }}) commonly known as *FLIR1*, *Gimbal* and *Go Fast* amplified this argument. While TTSA neither recorded nor originally created the footage, it helped organise the public release of the videos alongside testimony from former officials and aviators. As a result, discussion shifted from speculation about UFOs towards questions such as:

## Why the Navy changed its reporting procedures

The Navy's reporting reforms emerged from several overlapping pressures rather than from TTSA alone.

Operationally, pilots had continued reporting repeated encounters in military training areas. Institutionally, senior officials wanted more consistent collection of information about unidentified aircraft and unmanned systems operating near sensitive facilities. Politically, Congress had begun requesting briefings on the issue. Public attention generated by media reporting and TTSA's advocacy increased pressure to demonstrate that reports were being handled systematically rather than informally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/us-navy-ufo-sighting-guidelines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[history.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">us navy ufo sighting guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reporting UFO Sightings to the US Navy Just Got Easier &#124; HISTORYApril 25, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 25, 2019</span></span></span>

When the Navy announced its revised procedures in 2019, officials emphasised that the objective was data collection. They described the new guidance as facilitating objective, data-driven analysis and reducing the stigma that might discourage personnel from reporting unusual incidents. Importantly, the language focused on "unidentified aircraft" and "unmanned aerial systems" rather than extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wtop.com/government/2019/05/changes-for-navy-pilots-reporting-ufos-follow-strange-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wtop.com">[WTOP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wtop.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News‘Remove the stigma’: Navy changing its guidelines for reporting UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WTOP News‘Remove the stigma’: Navy changing its guidelines for reporting UFOs - WTOP News...</span></span></span>

This emphasis closely matched TTSA's public messaging, even though the Navy never credited the organisation with designing or directing the reforms.

## What can and cannot be credited to TTSA

A careful assessment distinguishes between influence and authorship.

### What TTSA can reasonably be credited with

TTSA helped normalise discussion of military UAP reports in several ways:

* It provided a prominent public platform for former defence and intelligence officials who argued that the issue should be treated professionally.
* It brought authentic Navy videos and pilot testimony into mainstream news coverage.
* It consistently framed unexplained encounters as matters of aviation safety and national security rather than simply entertainment or conspiracy.

## Reporting reform as TTSA's most lasting policy impact

TTSA's broader scientific ambitions remain debated, and many of its more ambitious technological claims have not been independently validated. Its most durable institutional legacy lies elsewhere.

By helping shift the conversation from "belief in UFOs" to "reporting unidentified aerial phenomena as potential flight-safety and security hazards", TTSA contributed to a lasting change in how military encounters were discussed. The Navy's 2019 reporting reforms reflected that new framing by encouraging structured reporting and objective analysis instead of informal handling or cultural dismissal.
