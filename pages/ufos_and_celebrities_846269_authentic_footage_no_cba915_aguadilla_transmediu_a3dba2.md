## Introduction

The 2013 infrared video recorded from a U.S. Customs and Border Protection aircraft near Aguadilla, [Puerto Rico]({{ 'puerto-rico/' | relative_url }}), is often presented as one of the strongest examples of a "transmedium" unidentified object—something that appeared to fly through the air, enter the sea without slowing, emerge again and finally split into two. If those observations reflected the object's true motion, they would represent behaviour unlike any known aircraft.

However, later [reconstructions]({{ 'reconstructions/' | relative_url }}) reached a very different conclusion. Investigators who modelled the aircraft's flight path, sensor geometry, weather and viewing conditions found that the apparent extraordinary manoeuvres could be explained without requiring an object to enter the ocean at all. ## Did the Aguadilla UFO Really Enter the Ocean?

The strongest claim made about the Aguadilla video was that it showed an object travelling seamlessly between air and water—a capability commonly described as "transmedium". This interpretation became influential because the infrared recording appears to show a bright object reaching the shoreline, disappearing into the sea and later reappearing.

Subsequent trajectory reconstruction challenged that interpretation. By combining aircraft navigation data with modelling of the infrared sensor's line of sight, investigators concluded that the object's projected path remained over land rather than crossing into the ocean. According to this reconstruction, the apparent sea entry resulted from the viewing geometry rather than the object's actual position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>

This distinction is critical. Infrared video records a two-dimensional projection from a moving aircraft. Without independently measured range, an object that appears to cross a coastline in the image may actually remain inland when reconstructed in three-dimensional space.

## The Apparent Split and Sea Entry

Another dramatic feature of the recording is the apparent division of one object into two.

Early interpretations treated this as evidence that a single craft physically separated into two independent objects. Later analysis instead argued that the video had captured two closely spaced objects throughout much of the encounter. As the Customs and Border Protection aircraft climbed and circled the area, the infrared camera's changing viewing angle increasingly resolved them as separate targets instead of one merged image. Investigators identified several earlier moments in the recording where the pair briefly become distinguishable before appearing merged again, supporting the interpretation that two objects were present all along.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>

The apparent entry into the ocean received a similar reinterpretation. Rather than submerging, the objects were assessed as gradually losing thermal contrast against the background while the aircraft's distance from them increased substantially. Infrared cameras do not simply record visible outlines; they depend on temperature contrast. When that contrast diminishes, an object can appear to fade into its surroundings even though it remains in view geometrically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>

## Aircraft Path and Cloud Obscuration

The motion of the observing aircraft is central to understanding why the video appears so unusual.

During the encounter, the Customs and Border Protection aircraft flew an arcing path around Rafael Hernández Airport while climbing by more than 1,700 feet. At the same time, the range between the aircraft and the observed objects nearly tripled. These changes continuously altered the viewing geometry recorded by the infrared sensor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>

The reconstruction also found that the aircraft entered scattered cloud layers during the latter part of the recording. Partial cloud obscuration, increasing distance and changing thermal conditions all reduced image quality near the end of the video. What viewers interpreted as an object repeatedly entering and exiting the water could instead result from intermittent loss of thermal contrast combined with obscuration along the sensor's line of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>

An additional optical factor is motion parallax. Because the observing aircraft was itself moving rapidly while changing direction, slow-moving distant objects could appear to traverse the landscape much faster than they actually were. Motion parallax is a familiar effect when viewing scenery from a moving vehicle: nearby objects seem to race past while distant ones appear to drift. Applied to airborne infrared imagery, the same geometry can create an illusion of exceptional speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-case-resolution-reports-2023-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Resolves Aguadilla Sphere as Sky Lanterns, DoD / All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) · 2023 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Remains Discussed

The Aguadilla video continues to attract debate because earlier independent analyses reconstructed the event differently and argued for much higher speeds, complex manoeuvres and genuine water entry. Those differing conclusions arose largely from different assumptions about the object's distance, the camera geometry and the available flight data.

The case therefore illustrates a broader lesson about interpreting authentic UFO recordings. The recording itself is genuine, but extraordinary conclusions depend on accurately reconstructing where the object actually was in three-dimensional space. Small changes in assumptions about range, viewing angle or sensor behaviour can transform an apparently impossible flight profile into one consistent with ordinary objects drifting with the wind. That is why the Aguadilla incident is frequently cited as an example of how [authentic footage]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) alone cannot establish that an object exhibited exotic technology or extraterrestrial origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-2020s-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution-2025-aaro-puerto-rico-uap-case-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 2020s aaro puerto rico uap case resolution 2025 aaro puerto rico uap case resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyAARO Aguadilla Resolution Report, March 2025, All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), U.S. Department of Defense · 2...</span></span></span>
