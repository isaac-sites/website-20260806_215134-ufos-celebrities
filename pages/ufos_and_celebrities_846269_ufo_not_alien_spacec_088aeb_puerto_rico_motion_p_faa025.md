## Introduction

The infrared video recorded from a U.S. Customs and Border Protection aircraft near Rafael Hernández Airport, [Aguadilla]({{ 'aguadilla/' | relative_url }}), Puerto Rico, on 26 April 2013 became one of the best-known modern UFO cases because it appeared to show an object racing across the landscape, entering the sea, emerging again, and even splitting into two. For years, those apparent manoeuvres were cited as evidence of technology beyond known aircraft.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_ufo_not_alien_spacec_088aeb_puerto_rico_motion_p_faa025-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Puerto Rico illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_ufo_not_alien_spacec_088aeb_puerto_rico_motion_p_faa025-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufos_and_celebrities_846269_ufo_not_alien_spacec_088aeb_puerto_rico_motion_p_faa025-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})), however, concluded that the dramatic appearance was largely created by viewing geometry rather than extraordinary flight. By reconstructing the aircraft's position, the infrared sensor's orientation and the local wind conditions, investigators found that the apparent extreme speed resulted from motion parallax—a well-understood optical effect that can make slow-moving objects appear to move rapidly when viewed from a fast-moving aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_Puerto_Rico_UAP_Case_Resolution.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Puerto Rico UAP Case Resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Puerto Rico UAP Case ResolutionMarch 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the Footage Appeared to Show

The original infrared recording seemed to depict several remarkable behaviours:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* An object moving at very high speed over Puerto Rico.
* A descent towards the coastline.
* An apparent entry into the water followed by re-emergence.
* A single object separating into two identical objects before disappearing offshore.

</div>

Viewed without detailed positional data, these observations appeared difficult to reconcile with ordinary aircraft or balloons. Because the video lacked obvious depth cues, many viewers naturally interpreted the object's motion relative to the ground as its true speed.

The infrared imagery also contributed to the mystery. Thermal cameras record heat differences rather than visible light, making it difficult to judge size, distance and altitude from appearance alone. A small, distant object can resemble a much larger nearby one if its actual range is unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## How Motion Parallax Changed the Scene

Motion parallax is an everyday visual phenomenon. As an observer moves, nearby objects appear to sweep rapidly across the background while distant objects seem to move much more slowly. From a fast-moving aircraft using a zoomed infrared sensor, this effect can become especially pronounced.

According to AARO's reconstruction, three factors combined to create the illusion of exceptional speed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The aircraft itself was travelling quickly.** Its forward motion continuously changed the viewing angle.
* **The infrared sensor was highly magnified.** Small changes in line of sight translated into large apparent movements across the image.
* **The object was relatively distant and drifting slowly.** Against the fixed landscape, the changing observation geometry made it appear to race over the terrain.

</div>

The result was an apparent ground speed far greater than the object's actual movement. Instead of exhibiting extraordinary acceleration, the reconstructed flight path indicated movement consistent with prevailing winds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_Puerto_Rico_UAP_Case_Resolution.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Puerto Rico UAP Case Resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Puerto Rico UAP Case ResolutionMarch 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why Reconstructed Geometry Altered the Conclusion

Rather than analysing the video frame by frame in isolation, investigators reconstructed the event using multiple sources of information.

The reconstruction incorporated:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* the Customs and Border Protection aircraft's recorded position and flight path;
* the infrared sensor's elevation, azimuth and viewing angles;
* Digital Systems Toolkit (STK) modelling to recreate the camera geometry;
* local meteorological data.

</div>

With those inputs combined, AARO concluded that the observed objects travelled in a straight line at approximately 3.6 metres per second (about 8 mph), closely matching measured winds of roughly 4.4 metres per second from the east-northeast. The dramatic apparent speed was therefore attributed to motion parallax rather than unusual propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_Puerto_Rico_UAP_Case_Resolution.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Puerto Rico UAP Case Resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Puerto Rico UAP Case ResolutionMarch 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2025</span></span></span>

The reconstruction also changed other aspects of the interpretation. Instead of one object dividing into two, investigators concluded that two separate objects had been travelling near one another throughout the observation. As the sensor's changing angle intermittently resolved both targets, they briefly appeared to merge and then separate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/SASC_AARO_Open_Hearing_Case_Slides_19Nov2024.pdf?ver=Hec1SwKwP-4zSRJwIEIlJw%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Open Hearing Case Slides: Senate Armed Services Subcommittee on Emerging Threats and Capabilities...</span></span></span>

## Why the Object Never Entered the Water

One of the video's most striking moments appeared to show the object diving beneath the ocean surface before emerging again, suggesting so-called "transmedium" travel.

AARO's reconstruction reached a different conclusion. The model indicated that the objects remained over land throughout the recording, following a straight path consistent with wind drift. The apparent disappearance occurred because the infrared contrast between the objects and the background diminished as the viewing geometry changed, creating the illusion that they had entered the sea. Investigators concluded that the objects never actually penetrated the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/SASC_AARO_Open_Hearing_Case_Slides_19Nov2024.pdf?ver=Hec1SwKwP-4zSRJwIEIlJw%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Open Hearing Case Slides: Senate Armed Services Subcommittee on Emerging Threats and Capabilities...</span></span></span>

## What the Puerto Rico Case Demonstrates

The [Puerto Rico video]({{ 'puerto-rico-video/' | relative_url }}) has become a useful illustration of why an unidentified object is not automatically evidence of advanced technology. Without accurate information about camera position, target distance and sensor orientation, even experienced observers can overestimate speed and misinterpret motion.

AARO's assessment does not rely on dismissing the original recording as fabricated. Instead, it argues that the same video looks fundamentally different once its geometry is reconstructed. In this interpretation, the apparent rapid acceleration, water entry and object splitting all emerge from perspective effects and changing sensor geometry rather than extraordinary flight characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_Puerto_Rico_UAP_Case_Resolution.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Puerto Rico UAP Case Resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Puerto Rico UAP Case ResolutionMarch 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2025</span></span></span>

Within the broader discussion of why a UFO does not necessarily imply an alien spacecraft, the Puerto Rico incident stands as a clear example of how incomplete spatial information can transform an ordinary aerial object into what appears, at first glance, to be an extraordinary one.
