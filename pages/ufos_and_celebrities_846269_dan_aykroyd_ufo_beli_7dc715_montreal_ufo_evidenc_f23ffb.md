## Introduction

[Dan Aykroyd]({{ 'dan-aykroyd/' | relative_url }}) has described one particular UFO sighting in Montreal as the experience that most strongly reinforced his belief that some unidentified aerial objects cannot be explained by conventional aircraft. Unlike his briefer accounts of other sightings, this incident includes a relatively detailed description of the object's appearance, movement and viewing conditions, and he has consistently emphasised that another person witnessed it with him. At the same time, the case illustrates the limits of eyewitness testimony. No publicly available photographs, radar data, official investigation, hotel records or contemporaneous witness statements have emerged to corroborate what Aykroyd remembers. As a result, the Montreal incident remains an intriguing personal account rather than a documented UFO case that independent investigators can reconstruct or verify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.houstonpress.com/arts/dan-aykroyd-on-ufos-and-the-wonder-of-whats-out-there/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: houstonpress.com">[Houston Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">houstonpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Houston Press Dan Aykroyd on UFOs and the Wonder of What’s Out There</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Houston PressDan Aykroyd on UFOs and the Wonder of What’s Out There - Houston PressOctober 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span>

Across multiple interviews, Aykroyd has given a broadly consistent account. He says he was staying in a high-floor room at a Montreal hotel overlooking the St Lawrence River when a companion drew his attention to an unusual object outside the window. ## The Second Witness Adds Context—but Not Independent Confirmation

One reason Aykroyd considers the Montreal event especially persuasive is that he says he was not alone. According to his account, another person in the hotel room noticed the object first, and both watched it together before leaving the room to continue observing it from the corridor as it drifted away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/spectacular-hollywood-actor-claims-he-spotted-ufos-on-4-different-occasions-4835645" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndtv.com">[www.ndtv.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndtv.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ndtv.com.</span></span></span>

From an evidential perspective, a second witness is valuable because it reduces the likelihood that the observation resulted solely from an individual perceptual error. However, that advantage has practical limits in this case:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* the second witness has not publicly provided an independent detailed account;
* there is no known recorded interview made close to the time of the event;
* no signed statement has entered the public record;
* there is no opportunity to compare two independently preserved recollections for consistency.

</div>

Consequently, investigators have only Aykroyd's retrospective description of what both observers experienced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unexplained-mysteries.com/news/374032/dan-aykroyd-recalls-encounter-with-spectacular-ufo-in-montreal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unexplained-mysteries.com">[Unexplained Mysteries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unexplained-mysteries.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on unexplained-mysteries.com.</span></span></span>

## What the Reported Shape and Motion Can Establish

If Aykroyd's recollection is accurate, it establishes only what he believed he observed from his viewing position—not the true nature of the object.

Several features make the account unusual:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the reported lack of audible engine or rotor noise despite apparent proximity;
* an apparently stable hover;
* a slow, controlled departure rather than rapid acceleration;
* the distinctive rounded structures underneath.

</div>

These characteristics explain why Aykroyd concluded that the object was unlike any conventional aircraft familiar to him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/spectacular-hollywood-actor-claims-he-spotted-ufos-on-4-different-occasions-4835645" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndtv.com">[www.ndtv.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndtv.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ndtv.com.</span></span></span>

However, none of those observations is sufficient on its own to identify an object's origin. Human observers estimating distance, size and speed from a single viewpoint can easily make substantial errors because those quantities depend heavily on knowing the object's actual range. Without an independently measured distance, even large differences in estimated dimensions or velocity cannot be resolved confidently. Modern scientific approaches to unidentified aerial phenomena therefore emphasise combining visual observations with calibrated imagery, radar, acoustic measurements and other sensor data rather than relying solely on eyewitness impressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientific Investigation of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Using Multimodal Ground-Based ObservatoriesMay 29, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Cannot Be Independently Verified

The principal limitation of the Montreal sighting is not that Aykroyd's account is internally inconsistent. Rather, it is that virtually every form of external evidence normally used to investigate unusual aerial events is absent.

Publicly available material does not include:

* a precise date and time;
* confirmation of the exact hotel room or viewing location;
* photographs or video;
* radar data;
* air traffic documentation linked to the event;
* weather analysis specific to the observation;
* police, aviation or military reports;
* independent testimony from additional observers.

</div>

## Plausible Explanations That Cannot Be Ruled In or Out

Because the available evidence consists almost entirely of retrospective eyewitness testimony, neither extraordinary nor ordinary explanations can be confirmed.
