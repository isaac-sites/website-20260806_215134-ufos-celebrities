## Introduction

The 2015 **GoFast** video is one of the three US Navy infrared recordings that became central to public debate over unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), especially after their promotion through media coverage and figures such as Tom DeLonge and [To the Stars Academy]({{ 'to-the-stars/' | relative_url }}). At first glance, the footage appears to show a small object racing just above the ocean at extraordinary speed. However, detailed analysis by the US government's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) and the NASA UAP Independent Study Team reached a different conclusion: the apparent extreme motion is largely a **parallax illusion**, created by the geometry between a fast-moving fighter aircraft, its targeting camera and a much more distant object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

Rather than demonstrating exotic flight characteristics, the available telemetry embedded in the video indicates that the object was thousands of feet above the sea and moving at a speed consistent with ordinary winds at that altitude. ## Was GoFast Really Skimming the Ocean?

The first impression created by the footage is compelling. The infrared targeting pod tracks a small object while the ocean fills the background, making it appear as though the target is only metres above the waves. Crew members can be heard reacting with surprise as they acquire the object.

That visual impression is misleading because the video compresses a three-dimensional scene into a two-dimensional image. The camera is mounted on a fighter aircraft travelling at several hundred miles per hour while viewing a distant target at a shallow downward angle. Without reconstructing the geometry from the telemetry displayed on the screen, the eye naturally interprets the target as being much closer to the water than it actually is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important because the extraordinary claim surrounding GoFast was never simply that an unidentified object existed, but that it appeared to be flying at remarkable speed just above the sea surface. The altitude estimate fundamentally changes that interpretation.

## What Viewers Think the Video Shows

The illusion arises because several visual cues encourage an incorrect interpretation.

Many viewers assume:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object is almost touching the ocean;
* the background water is stationary while the object races across it;
* the object's apparent sideways movement reflects its true speed.

</div>

In reality, the background itself appears to slide beneath the target because the observing aircraft is moving rapidly and changing its viewing angle. The camera continuously compensates to keep the target near the centre of the frame, while the ocean sweeps past underneath. This makes the object appear to streak over the surface even if its own motion is relatively modest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

Parallax is a familiar phenomenon in everyday life. From a moving train, nearby trees seem to rush past while distant hills barely move. The GoFast video presents a more complicated version of the same effect because both the observing aircraft and its stabilised targeting system are in motion.

## How Altitude and Viewing Geometry Change the Estimate

The key evidence comes not from the image itself but from the numerical information permanently displayed on the targeting-pod recording.

NASA's Independent Study Team showed that the displayed range to the target, the aircraft altitude and the camera's depression angle allow straightforward trigonometric reconstruction of the encounter. Their calculation places the object at roughly **13,000 feet above sea level**, not immediately above the ocean surface. The same reconstruction indicates that the object was approximately **4.2 miles beyond the point on the ocean that appears directly beneath it in the image**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

Once that geometry is established, the apparent high speed largely disappears.

Using the changing bearing of the target over approximately 22 seconds, together with the aircraft's own speed and turn, NASA estimated that the object's average ground speed was about **40 mph (roughly 35 knots)**. That is comparable to wind speeds commonly encountered at that altitude rather than the hundreds or thousands of miles per hour suggested by casual viewing. The report notes uncertainties in the calculation but concludes that the available data do not require any extraordinary velocity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

Another observation supports this interpretation. In the infrared display settings used during the encounter, the object appears colder than the surrounding ocean rather than exhibiting obvious thermal signatures expected from a powerful propulsion system. While this does not identify the object, it provides no evidence for unusually energetic flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## What the GoFast Case Demonstrates

The GoFast analysis illustrates an important lesson for interpreting military sensor footage. Human intuition is poorly suited to estimating the motion of distant objects viewed through stabilised infrared cameras mounted on fast-moving aircraft. Telemetry, geometry and sensor metadata are often more informative than the visual impression alone.

Within the wider discussion of the Pentagon UFO videos, GoFast therefore serves as a case where careful reconstruction substantially changed the interpretation. The footage remains an authentic Navy recording of an unidentified object at the time of observation, but the best-supported technical analyses indicate that its seemingly dramatic speed was an artefact of perspective rather than evidence of extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>
