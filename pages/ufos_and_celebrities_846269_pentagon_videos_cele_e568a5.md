## Introduction

The famous “Pentagon UFO videos” confirmed something important but limited: the recordings were genuine US Navy footage, captured by military sensor systems during encounters that officials had not identified at the time. They did **not** confirm alien spacecraft, impossible technology or even that every apparent movement in the clips represented the object’s real motion. The three videos came from two periods of US Navy activity. FLIR1 was recorded in November 2004 during the encounter associated with the USS *Nimitz* carrier group. ## What official authentication meant

On 27 April 2020, the Department of Defense authorised the release of all three videos. Its statement was carefully framed. It confirmed that they were unclassified Navy recordings made in 2004 and 2015, said their release would not expose sensitive capabilities or interfere with later investigations, and explained that publication was intended to clear up misconceptions about whether the circulating footage was genuine or whether additional material had been hidden within the clips. It concluded that the recorded phenomena remained “unidentified”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Press-Products/%20Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">statement by the department of defense on the release of historical navy videos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarStatement by the Department of Defense on the Release of Historical Navy Videos &gt; U.S. Department of War &gt; Press Product...</span></span></span>(https://www.war.gov/News/Press-Products/%20Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/)

That authentication established several points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The clips were not fabricated fan videos or computer-generated hoaxes.
* They originated in US Navy aviation operations.
* The cockpit displays and voices belonged to real military recordings.
* Officials had not publicly assigned a confirmed identity to the objects shown.
* The Pentagon considered the released versions safe to publish without exposing protected technical information.

</div>

None of those findings determined what the objects were. “Unidentified” described the status of the available assessment, not a physical category. It did not mean extraterrestrial, technologically revolutionary or incapable of ordinary explanation.

The distinction is especially important because a sensor recording is not a transparent window onto an event. Infrared targeting systems translate heat contrast into an image, stabilise the picture and track targets while both the aircraft and sensor head are moving. Apparent shape, speed and rotation can therefore depend on range, viewing geometry, image processing, glare and the motion of the observing aircraft. A real military video can authentically record a misleading visual impression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/Information%20Papers/AARO_Effect_of_Forced_Perspective_and_Parallax_View_on_UAP_Observations_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span></span></span>

Official language was often simplified as it moved through headlines and celebrity commentary. “The Navy confirms the videos are real” could easily become “the Navy confirms UFOs are real”, and then “the Pentagon admits extraordinary craft are real”. The first formulation is broadly accurate if “real” refers to the recordings. The second is true only in the limited sense that military personnel sometimes record objects they cannot immediately identify. The third adds a conclusion the Pentagon did not make.

DeLonge himself promoted the 2020 decision as a major vindication. He told *Rolling Stone* that the Navy and Pentagon had acknowledged that the videos and objects were real, characterised the development as something his team had helped force into public view, and predicted much more consequential disclosure. His enthusiasm illustrates the gap between official and promotional framing: the Department of Defense authenticated recordings and retained an unresolved label, while DeLonge presented the moment as confirmation of a much larger UFO narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://au.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/tom-delonge-ufo-video-pentagon-release-blink-182-10815/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: au.rollingstone.com">[Rolling Stone Australia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">au.rollingstone.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rollingstone.com.</span></span></span>

Celebrity promotion did not create the underlying incidents. It did, however, affect which interpretation reached the public first and most forcefully. Most viewers did not encounter the clips through a technical explanation of infrared sensors, [missing metadata]({{ 'missing-metadata/' | relative_url }}) and uncertainty ranges. They encountered them as “UFO videos”, frequently accompanied by claims from former officials that the objects displayed capabilities beyond known aircraft.

## Why the clips do not prove extraordinary craft

Each video presents a different analytical problem, and the footage alone supplies less information than its dramatic reputation implies.

**GoFast** appears at first glance to show a small object racing just above the sea. The visual effect is compelling because the background moves rapidly beneath it. In 2025, the Pentagon’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, or [AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }}), published a detailed reconstruction using values visible on the display. It assessed that the object was approximately 13,000 feet above the ocean, not skimming the surface, and was travelling somewhere between about 5 and 92 miles per hour. AARO could not identify it, but concluded that it showed no anomalous performance. Its apparently extreme speed was largely a parallax effect created by viewing an object from a fast-moving aircraft against a distant background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_GoFast_Case_Resolution_Card_Methodology_Final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Defense Case: “Go Fast” Case Resolution &#124; February 6, 2025 Case Overview In January 2015, a U.S. Navy F/A-18F pil...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 6, 2025</span></span></span>

The finding is revealing because GoFast was widely promoted on the basis of exactly the characteristic later weakened by analysis: its apparent velocity. The case remained unidentified at the object level, yet the extraordinary-performance claim did not survive geometric reconstruction. An unknown object travelling at an ordinary speed is evidentially different from a vehicle demonstrating impossible propulsion.

**Gimbal** shows a dark infrared form that appears to rotate while aircrew react to it. Advocates have treated the apparent rotation, absence of visible wings and reported accompanying radar contacts as signs of unconventional technology. A competing interpretation is that the rotating form is infrared glare associated with a distant heat source and that its apparent turn is linked to the targeting pod’s optical and mechanical behaviour. Public analysis remains contested, partly because the released clip lacks the complete supporting radar, aircraft-position and original sensor data needed to reconstruct the event conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08773" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

The disagreement does not justify selecting the most exotic explanation. It shows why a short processed clip cannot independently establish an object’s shape or flight characteristics. Even analyses arguing that Gimbal may represent unusual movement rely on assumed distance, pilot recollections and information outside the public video. Alternative jet-and-glare models also depend on assumptions. The footage remains interesting, but its ambiguity is the central fact rather than a technical obstacle that publicity can simply overlook.

**FLIR1**, associated with the 2004 *Nimitz* encounter, is likewise only one piece of a broader reported event. Pilot testimony, shipboard radar accounts and the targeting-pod clip are often combined into a single narrative about a white “Tic Tac” object. The released video itself does not show the full encounter described by witnesses, nor does it contain enough public information to establish extraordinary acceleration. Treating every element of the broader story as though it were visibly demonstrated in the clip overstates what the recording contains.

This separation between footage and surrounding testimony is crucial. A pilot may have had a vivid visual encounter; radar operators may have tracked unusual returns; and a targeting pod may later have recorded an object. Whether those observations all involved the same object, how accurately their times and positions can be aligned, and whether the available data exclude ordinary causes are additional questions. The short videos cannot answer them by themselves.

## The missing data behind the spectacle

Military provenance can make footage seem more complete than it is. In reality, the publicly available files lack much of what investigators would want for a firm identification: original uncompressed sensor data, complete metadata, exact aircraft tracks, full-length recordings, radar records, weather information, nearby flight activity and calibrated measurements from independent viewpoints.

AARO’s GoFast analysis demonstrates both the value and the limits of the surviving display information. Analysts could estimate a plausible altitude and speed range from numbers visible in the clip, but the original file and associated metadata were no longer available. The aircraft’s exact location and heading were also unknown, preventing a single definitive trajectory from being calculated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/case_resolution_reports/AARO_GoFast_Case_Resolution_Card_Methodology_Final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Defense Case: “Go Fast” Case Resolution &#124; February 6, 2025 Case Overview In January 2015, a U.S. Navy F/A-18F pil...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 6, 2025</span></span></span>

Those gaps matter because apparent anomalies are often produced by uncertainty in distance. Without reliable range, a small nearby object and a large distant object can occupy the same angular size in an image. Without a stable external reference, the observer’s own motion can make a slow object appear fast. AARO has highlighted forced perspective and parallax as recurring sources of mistaken estimates of size, position and velocity in UAP reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/Information%20Papers/AARO_Effect_of_Forced_Perspective_and_Parallax_View_on_UAP_Observations_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span></span></span>

NASA’s independent UAP study reached a compatible broader conclusion. It found that intriguing observations usually lack the consistent, calibrated data required for definitive scientific judgements. The report called for standardised reporting, robust metadata and multiple forms of measurement rather than relying on isolated videos or eyewitness impressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report-0.pdf?emrc=69fb3c3d5dcea" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap independent study team final report 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">uap independent study team final report 0</span></span></span>

This does not mean the Navy encounters were trivial or unworthy of investigation. Unidentified objects near military training areas can represent flight hazards, surveillance devices, balloons, drones, sensor problems or misunderstood natural phenomena. Establishing which explanation applies has genuine defence and aviation value. The point is narrower: military concern about an unidentified incursion is not evidence that the incursion was extraterrestrial.

## How celebrity promotion changed the meaning

DeLonge’s role was significant because he did more than repeat an existing news story. He helped build a public-facing organisation around former officials who could obtain attention from mainstream journalists and policymakers. His fame reduced the usual barrier between specialised UFO advocacy and mass entertainment, while his longstanding interest made the campaign appear personally authentic rather than manufactured by a publicity firm.

To the Stars also supplied a ready-made narrative for the videos. The footage was not presented merely as unresolved aviation data. It appeared within a larger programme involving alleged government secrecy, advanced technology, insider knowledge and the possibility that extraordinary craft had been observed for decades. The organisation’s entertainment activities reinforced that framing even when individual claims remained unproved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.entrepreneur.com/leadership/the-former-singer-of-blink-182-is-creating-a-sci-fi-disney/306267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: entrepreneur.com">[Entrepreneur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">entrepreneur.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Former Singer of Blink-182 Is Creating a Sci-Fi Disney for Millennials (and It Studies UFOs, Too)December 18, 2017 — The...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2017</span></span></span>

The resulting publicity had constructive effects. It helped normalise discussion of unusual encounters among military aviators, drew attention to weaknesses in reporting systems and encouraged government bodies to treat unidentified incursions as a legitimate safety and intelligence issue. Navy officials said they wanted to reduce stigma so that pilots would report unexplained objects rather than remain silent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/navy-confirms-ufo-videos-real" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Navy Confirms UFO Videos Are Real and Show Unidentified Aerial Phenomena &#124; HISTORYNavy Confirms UFO Videos Are Real and Show Unide...</span></span></span>

But the same campaign repeatedly blurred three separate propositions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **The recordings are authentic.**
2. **The recorded objects were not identified from the available evidence.**
3. **The objects were vehicles with extraordinary or non-human origins.**

</div>

The Pentagon confirmed the first and retained the second. It did not confirm the third.

Celebrity-led promotion made that distinction harder to maintain because publicity rewards a simple breakthrough story. “Government authenticates previously leaked Navy footage” is precise but qualified. “Pentagon confirms UFOs” is shorter and more shareable. “Musician helps force alien disclosure” is more dramatic still. Each step adds meaning that was not contained in the official statement.

The videos therefore offer a useful lesson in evaluating celebrity UFO advocacy. A famous promoter can be instrumental in locating witnesses, attracting journalists and pressuring institutions to release information. Those achievements affect access and attention, not the physical interpretation of the evidence. The identity and behaviour of an object must still be established through measurements, metadata and analysis that stand independently of the promoter’s confidence.

## What the Pentagon videos really confirmed

The enduring importance of FLIR1, Gimbal and GoFast is not that they proved alien visitation. They showed that trained military personnel sometimes encounter and record objects they cannot immediately identify; that official reporting and data retention were inadequate; and that footage from sophisticated sensors can remain ambiguous when crucial context is missing.

They also showed how rapidly evidential categories can collapse in popular culture. “Authentic recording”, “unidentified object” and “extraordinary craft” came to be treated as interchangeable, particularly when promoted by a celebrity-led organisation with former defence insiders and a compelling disclosure narrative.

Later analysis has made the distinction clearer. GoFast’s dramatic apparent speed can be explained largely through geometry, even though the object itself remains unidentified. Gimbal remains disputed because the public clip cannot settle whether its most striking features belong to the object or to the imaging system. FLIR1 does not independently demonstrate the full range of manoeuvres described in the broader *Nimitz* account. None provides verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

The responsible conclusion is neither that the videos are meaningless nor that they reveal alien machines. They are authentic but incomplete military records. Celebrity promotion helped bring them into public view and encouraged serious discussion of UAP reporting, but it also encouraged conclusions beyond what the recordings could prove.

  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
