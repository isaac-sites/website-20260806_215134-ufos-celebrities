## Introduction

Could standardised public reporting make unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP) sightings more useful? Potentially yes—but only if the reports collect consistent, measurable information rather than isolated stories. NASA's 2023 independent UAP study concluded that the main obstacle to scientific progress is not a lack of reports but a lack of comparable, high-quality data. Instead of treating each sighting as a unique anecdote, a structured reporting system could allow investigators to compare observations made by different people, combine them with weather, aviation and satellite records, and test competing explanations using reproducible methods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

This approach fits NASA's broader standard for stronger UAP research: improve the quality, calibration and transparency of evidence before drawing conclusions. Within a wider discussion that may include celebrity sightings or highly publicised [witness accounts]({{ 'witness-accounts/' | relative_url }}), standardised reporting shifts attention away from who made the claim and towards what information was actually recorded.

## Can smartphone reports turn sightings into usable data?

Modern smartphones contain far more than cameras. They automatically record metadata including time, date and GPS position, while many also include orientation sensors, accelerometers and compass data. NASA's study recommended exploring crowdsourced smartphone-based reporting because these devices could provide consistent digital records from many independent observers rather than disconnected eyewitness recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

A standard reporting platform would not assume every observation represents something extraordinary. Instead, it would ask every observer to collect the same core information so investigators can compare reports directly. If several witnesses independently record the same event with synchronised timestamps and locations, researchers can estimate viewing geometry, compare camera perspectives and check whether the object matches known aircraft, satellites, balloons or atmospheric events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

This is a familiar scientific approach. Citizen-science projects in astronomy, meteor observation and wildlife monitoring already depend on thousands of participants submitting observations in consistent formats. Individual reports may have limited value, but large numbers of standardised observations can reveal patterns that isolated accounts cannot.

## Information every public UAP report should capture

A reporting system becomes useful only if every submission contains enough contextual information for later analysis. NASA's recommendations and guidance from the US All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) point towards collecting structured metadata alongside images or videos rather than relying solely on written descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

Useful information includes:

* **Precise date and time**, preferably generated automatically by the device.
* **GPS location** of the observer.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Viewing direction**, including approximate compass bearing and elevation.
* **Duration** of the observation.
* **Original photographs or video**, not compressed copies.
* **Camera metadata**, including focal length, exposure settings and resolution where available.
* **Weather and visibility**, including cloud cover if known.
* **Observer movement**, such as standing still, walking or travelling in a vehicle or aircraft.
* **Other witnesses** present at the same location.
* **Any unusual sounds, lights or apparent motion**, described separately from interpretations.

</div>

AARO similarly notes that metadata from mobile-phone images, accurate timestamps, GPS information and higher-resolution recordings are substantially more valuable than edited media or recollections recorded much later. It also highlights the usefulness of independently recorded radio-frequency measurements where available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

Standardisation also makes automated quality checks possible. Reports with missing timestamps, altered images or incomplete metadata can be flagged before investigators spend time analysing them.

## How multiple observers can provide independent corroboration

The greatest scientific advantage of standardised reporting is not obtaining more reports but obtaining independent reports that can be compared mathematically.

If several observers submit reports of the same event from different locations, investigators may be able to:

* determine whether everyone observed the same object;
* estimate altitude through [triangulation]({{ 'triangulation/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* reconstruct the object's apparent path;
* compare different camera recordings against one another;
* eliminate explanations that are inconsistent with the combined geometry.

</div>

Because each report is submitted independently using the same format, agreement between observers becomes measurable rather than anecdotal.

A practical example comes from aviation rather than a public reporting app. Researchers investigating a 2022 Pacific sighting compared reports from multiple airline crews with photographs, flight-path data and satellite orbital information. The reconstruction showed that the apparently mysterious object was consistent with a recently launched Starlink satellite train viewed under unusual lighting conditions. The case illustrates how multiple independent observations become far more informative when combined with accurate timing and external datasets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Enhancing Space Situational Awareness to Mitigate Risk: A Single-Case Study in the Misidentification of a Recently-Launched Starlink...</span></span></span>

Equally important, if several well-documented reports cannot be reconciled with known aircraft, satellites or atmospheric phenomena, investigators have a stronger basis for identifying genuinely [unresolved cases]({{ 'unresolved-cases/' | relative_url }}) deserving further study.

## Privacy, false reports and limits of smartphone sensors

Standardised reporting also creates governance challenges that must be addressed if public participation is to be sustainable.

### Protecting personal information

Location, timestamps and photographs can reveal sensitive personal information. A public reporting system therefore needs clear policies covering:

* how precise location data are stored;
* whether reports are anonymous or attributable;
* who can access [original files]({{ 'original-files/' | relative_url }});
* how long information is retained;
* whether researchers receive raw or anonymised data.

Without transparent privacy protections, many witnesses may hesitate to participate even if they observed something genuinely unusual.

### Reducing false or misleading reports

A structured form cannot eliminate fabricated submissions, but it can make them easier to identify.

Automatic consistency checks can compare reported weather against meteorological records, verify timestamps, examine image metadata and detect duplicate submissions. Reports that lack original files or contain inconsistent metadata can be separated from those supported by complete digital records.

Standardisation therefore improves data quality even when some inaccurate reports inevitably enter the system.

### Smartphone limitations

Smartphones remain imperfect scientific instruments.

Small image sensors struggle in low light, autofocus systems can misidentify distant objects, digital zoom reduces detail, and rolling shutters may distort fast-moving targets. Without a known distance, even high-quality video cannot reliably establish an object's size or speed. Optical effects, motion blur and perspective can all create misleading impressions. NASA therefore treats smartphone evidence as one source of information to be combined with radar, astronomical observations, aviation records and other calibrated measurements rather than as definitive proof on its own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP FAQs - NASA Science...</span></span></span>

## What standardised reporting could realistically achieve

Standardised public reporting is best understood as a way to improve evidence quality rather than increase the number of unexplained sightings. A larger database alone does not produce better science if the observations cannot be compared or independently verified.

By collecting consistent metadata, synchronising observations from [multiple witnesses]({{ 'multiple-witnesses/' | relative_url }}) and preserving original digital records, researchers can more efficiently match sightings against aircraft, satellites, balloons, astronomical objects and atmospheric phenomena. Many reports may become easier to explain, while the smaller number that remain unresolved would rest on a stronger evidential foundation.

Within NASA's framework for stronger UAP research, that is the principal goal. A reporting system succeeds not because it generates more mystery, but because it reduces ambiguity and allows independent investigators to evaluate the same evidence using transparent, repeatable methods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>
