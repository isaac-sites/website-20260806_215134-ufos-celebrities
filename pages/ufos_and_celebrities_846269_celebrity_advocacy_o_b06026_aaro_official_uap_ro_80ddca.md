## Introduction

The creation of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) in 2022 marked the biggest structural change in how the US Department of Defense investigates unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) since modern military reporting began. Rather than relying on temporary programmes, fragmented intelligence efforts or service-specific investigations, AARO was established as a permanent office responsible for collecting, analysing and attempting to resolve reports across air, space, maritime, undersea and transmedium environments. Its purpose was not to prove or disprove extraordinary claims, but to provide a single government body capable of coordinating investigations, reducing intelligence surprises and improving reporting standards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>

Within the broader story of [celebrity advocacy]({{ 'official-actions/' | relative_url }}) and official UAP action, AARO represents the point at which public attention translated into a durable institutional response. Public campaigns, congressional pressure and media coverage helped create political momentum, but AARO's significance lies in replacing scattered investigations with an enduring governance framework that Congress could oversee and the Department of Defense could resource over time.

## Why ad hoc investigations were no longer considered sufficient

Before AARO, responsibility for unusual military sightings was divided among multiple organisations with different priorities. Programmes such as the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) and the later Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group (AOIMSG) were either limited in scope or lacked the authority to coordinate information across the defence establishment.

Congress concluded that this fragmented approach created several problems:

* reports were collected using inconsistent procedures;
* information remained separated between military services and intelligence organisations;
* analysts often lacked access to all available sensor data;
* aviation-safety concerns could be treated differently from intelligence concerns; and

The National Defense Authorization Act directed the Department of Defense to establish AARO specifically to synchronise efforts across the military and intelligence community. The office's mandate extends beyond aircraft to any unidentified object or detection operating in, or capable of moving between, air, space, maritime and undersea domains when it could affect national security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Congressional-Press-Products/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Congressional/Press Products</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Congressional/Press Products...</span></span></span>

This represented a governance change more than a scientific breakthrough. Instead of asking whether any particular sighting was extraordinary, the government first sought to ensure that every report entered a consistent investigative system.

## How AARO centralised collection and analysis

AARO's most important innovation was to create a central process for receiving, integrating and analysing information that had previously remained scattered across organisations.

Rather than relying primarily on eyewitness testimony, AARO's investigations are intended to combine multiple sources wherever possible, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* military radar and other sensor systems;
* electro-optical and infrared imagery;
* satellite information where available;
* pilot reports;
* intelligence reporting; and
* contextual operational data.

</div>

Its published mission emphasises detecting, tracking, identifying, attributing and mitigating unidentified phenomena using standardised scientific and intelligence practices. The office's objective is to minimise technical and intelligence surprise rather than to confirm extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>

This emphasis on data integration reflects an important institutional shift. Earlier investigations often examined isolated incidents. AARO instead attempts to identify recurring patterns across multiple reports, allowing analysts to compare events occurring in different locations, services and operational contexts.

The office also publishes annual reports, reporting trends and selected case resolutions after appropriate review, creating a more regular flow of official information than existed under previous arrangements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Congressional-Press-Products/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Congressional/Press Products</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Congressional/Press Products...</span></span></span>

## Why standardised reporting mattered

One recurring complaint from military aviators before AARO was uncertainty about how unusual encounters should be reported. Pilots sometimes feared professional stigma or were unsure which chain of command should receive reports.

AARO sought to normalise reporting by supporting standardised procedures across the Department of Defense and by coordinating guidance issued to military services. Civilian aviation reporting also became more structured, with civilian pilots encouraged to report unusual observations through existing Federal Aviation Administration channels, from which relevant reports can reach AARO.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Home...</span></span></span>

Standardisation produces several practical benefits:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* reports are collected in more consistent formats;
* investigators can compare similar cases more effectively;
* missing information becomes easier to identify;
* statistical trends become more meaningful; and
* commanders receive clearer assessments of possible safety or intelligence risks.

</div>

Importantly, a report remaining "unidentified" no longer necessarily implies something extraordinary. AARO repeatedly states that many cases remain unresolved simply because available sensors collected insufficient information for confident identification rather than because the object demonstrated impossible behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Home...</span></span></span>

## Protected reporting channels and continuing trust problems

Another significant institutional change involved creating protected reporting mechanisms.

Congress directed AARO to establish secure channels allowing current and former government personnel, service members and authorised contractors with direct knowledge of historical UAP-related activities to provide information. These reports were intended both to support ongoing investigations and to inform AARO's historical review of past government activity. The reporting system was designed alongside existing legal protections governing privacy and whistleblower safeguards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3513171/the-department-of-defense-launches-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-web/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3513171/the-department-of-defense-launches-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office-web/)

However, creating reporting channels did not automatically resolve long-standing trust problems.

Several groups remain sceptical for different reasons:

* **Some current and former officials** have questioned whether witnesses feel sufficiently protected from professional consequences.

* **Many UFO researchers** believe the office has been overly cautious or insufficiently transparent.
* **AARO leadership**, by contrast, has consistently argued that conclusions must follow available evidence rather than public expectations and that classification rules limit what can be released immediately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2024/mar/22/ufologists-sean-kirkpatrick-pentagon-report-uaps" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian He quit heading the Pentagon&#x27;s UFO office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Now a report of his has shaken up ufologyMarch 22, 2024 — Sean Kirkpatrick, who led the Pentagon&#x27;s All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 22, 2024</span></span></span>

As a result, AARO has become both an investigative office and a focal point for broader debates about transparency, secrecy and public confidence.

## What changed in practice—and what did not

A common misunderstanding is that creating AARO represented official acceptance of extraordinary explanations for UAP. It did not.

Instead, AARO changed the process by which reports are handled.

The office introduced:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* permanent organisational responsibility;
* cross-domain coordination;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3100053/dod-announces-the-establishment-of-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* standardised reporting procedures;
* regular reporting to Congress;
* centralised analytical capability;
* publication of selected findings and resolved cases; and
* structured historical reviews of earlier government activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Congressional-Press-Products/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Congressional/Press Products</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Congressional/Press Products...</span></span></span>

Those procedural reforms are distinct from the conclusions reached in individual investigations.

Across its [public reporting]({{ 'public-reporting/' | relative_url }}), AARO has attributed many resolved cases to ordinary causes such as balloons, aircraft, satellites, drones, birds or sensor limitations, while acknowledging that some incidents remain unresolved because available data are insufficient for positive identification. ## Why AARO matters within the broader UAP debate

The lasting significance of AARO lies less in any single case than in the institutional framework it created.

Celebrity advocacy, media attention and congressional hearings helped elevate UAP into a mainstream policy issue, but AARO transformed that attention into a permanent government function. It established an office responsible for gathering reports across multiple operational domains, applying common analytical standards and providing regular accountability to Congress.

Whether future investigations ultimately identify previously unknown technologies, foreign surveillance systems, [sensor artefacts]({{ 'sensor-artefacts/' | relative_url }}) or additional conventional explanations, AARO has shifted official UAP investigations away from intermittent, compartmentalised inquiries and towards an ongoing system of governance built around coordinated collection, scientific analysis and institutional oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>

    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
