## Introduction

[Kurt Russell]({{ 'kurt-russell/' | relative_url }})'s account is most consistent with the **earlier moving V-shaped formation** reported across Arizona on the evening of 13 March 1997, not with the **later stationary lights** that became widely associated with military illumination flares. This distinction matters because the phrase *[Phoenix Lights]({{ 'phoenix-lights/' | relative_url }})* now refers to **two separate events** that occurred several hours apart but are often merged in popular retellings. Russell described seeing six lights in a disciplined V formation while approaching Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport and reporting them to air traffic control in real time. That description matches the earlier sequence of moving lights far more closely than the later row of nearly stationary lights recorded on video over the Estrella Mountains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/phoenix/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenix Lights &#124; NUFORCAugust 13, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1997</span></span></span>

## The two events grouped under one name

Although both occurred on the same evening, the Phoenix Lights consisted of two distinct episodes.

The **first event**, beginning around 8:15–8:30 p.m. Arizona time, involved reports from witnesses across a broad north-to-south path through the state. Observers described a formation of lights moving steadily overhead, often in a V or wedge shape. Some believed they were looking at individual lights flying together, while others perceived a single enormous object with lights embedded in it. Reports came from communities well north of Phoenix before the formation was seen nearer the city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/phoenix/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenix Lights &#124; NUFORCAugust 13, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1997</span></span></span>

The **second event**, around 10:00 p.m., consisted of a row or arc of bright lights visible southwest of Phoenix. These lights appeared largely stationary before gradually disappearing one by one. They were widely filmed, making this the part of the incident most familiar to the public today. The US Air Force later stated that these lights were illumination flares dropped during an A-10 training exercise over the Barry M. Goldwater Range.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/were-phoenix-lights-27-years-120058263.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What caused the Phoenix Lights? 28 years after the UFO drama, here&#x27;s what we knowMarch 13, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2024</span></span></span>

Because television news repeatedly showed video of the later lights, many later discussions blurred the distinction between the moving formation and the stationary lights, even though they differ in timing, appearance and the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosdex.com/documents/1997-phoenix-lights-primary-documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosdex.com">[Disclosdex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosdex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1997 phoenix lights primary documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1997 Phoenix Lights Primary Documents &#124; DisclosdexMarch 13, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 1997</span></span></span>

## Why Russell's account fits the earlier formation

Russell's public description aligns closely with the characteristics of the earlier event.

Several aspects are particularly significant:

* He described **six lights in a V formation**, matching numerous early witness descriptions of a moving formation rather than a stationary line of lights.
* He encountered the lights while **approaching Phoenix for landing**, placing his sighting during the earlier phase of the evening rather than the later period when many people were already watching stationary lights near the mountains.
* He recalled making an **immediate radio call to air traffic control**, treating the lights as unidentified nearby traffic rather than observing distant lights lingering over the horizon.
* His account emphasised the **formation itself**, not descending or fading lights, which became a defining feature of the later [flare videos]({{ 'flare-videos/' | relative_url }}).

These details correspond far more naturally with the earlier sequence reported from northern Arizona through Phoenix than with the later flare display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/phoenix/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenix Lights &#124; NUFORCAugust 13, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1997</span></span></span>

Importantly, Russell has never claimed that he witnessed the later stationary lights or that he observed military flares. His account concerns an unidentified formation encountered during flight operations.

## Why the flare explanation is not enough

The Air Force's [flare explanation]({{ 'evidence-limits-071e7a/' | relative_url }}) is directed primarily at the **later Phoenix-area lights**, not necessarily every report made that evening. According to the official account, A-10 aircraft released long-burning illumination flares over the Barry M. Goldwater Range, producing lights that slowly descended behind the Estrella Mountains. This explanation corresponds well with the later video recordings, in which lights remain nearly fixed relative to the landscape before disappearing sequentially.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/were-phoenix-lights-27-years-120058263.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[yahoo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What caused the Phoenix Lights? 28 years after the UFO drama, here&#x27;s what we knowMarch 13, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2024</span></span></span>

That does not automatically resolve the earlier moving formation.

The earlier reports differ in several important respects:

* witnesses described lights travelling across large portions of Arizona rather than remaining fixed over one location;
* reports came from widely separated communities over a significant period before the later videos were recorded;
* many observers reported apparent movement across the sky rather than the gradual descent expected of illumination flares.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/phoenix/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Phoenix Lights &#124; NUFORCAugust 13, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1997</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why debate has persisted. Some sceptical investigators argue that the earlier formation can also be explained by conventional aircraft flying in formation under night-time viewing conditions, with perspective causing observers to perceive a single immense object. ## What Russell's testimony does—and does not—establish

Russell's recollection strengthens the historical record for the **earlier moving formation** because it comes from an experienced pilot who says he reported the lights while they were being observed rather than reconstructing the event years later.

However, his testimony has clear limits. He did not identify what produced the lights, estimate their true distance with certainty, or provide evidence that they were a single structured craft. Likewise, his account neither confirms nor disproves the military explanation for the later stationary lights.

The most accurate reading is therefore a narrow one: Kurt Russell's report belongs to the earlier V-formation phase of the Phoenix Lights incident. It should not be treated as evidence about the later lights that were widely photographed and subsequently attributed by the Air Force to military illumination flares.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/news/were-phoenix-lights-27-years-120058263.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[yahoo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What caused the Phoenix Lights? 28 years after the UFO drama, here&#x27;s what we knowMarch 13, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2024</span></span></span>
