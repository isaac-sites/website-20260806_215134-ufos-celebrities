## Introduction

Glowing spirals and expanding luminous clouds are among the most convincing-looking UFO reports because they often appear unlike any familiar aircraft or astronomical object. In reality, one of the best-understood causes is the exhaust plume from a high-altitude rocket stage during twilight. Long after the rocket itself has become too small to see, its exhaust can remain brightly illuminated by the Sun while observers on the ground are already in darkness. As the gases expand into the near-vacuum of the upper atmosphere, they can form vast glowing clouds or striking spiral patterns that seem detached from any visible vehicle. This combination of unusual lighting, immense apparent size and unfamiliar motion has repeatedly triggered reports of mysterious craft, even among experienced observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

## How twilight keeps high-altitude exhaust illuminated

The key to the effect is geometry rather than anything unusual about the rocket itself.

Near sunrise or sunset, people on the ground stand within Earth's shadow, but a rocket climbing tens or even hundreds of kilometres above the surface can still be illuminated directly by the Sun. Exhaust released at those altitudes contains tiny particles of water vapour, carbon dioxide and other combustion products that rapidly cool, condense and often freeze. These particles scatter sunlight extremely efficiently against an otherwise dark sky, making the plume appear brilliantly luminous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

Because the illuminated exhaust is so much larger than the rocket, witnesses frequently lose sight of the vehicle entirely. What remains visible is a glowing cloud that appears to float independently across the sky, creating the impression that the light itself is the object.

The effect is largely restricted to launches occurring roughly within an hour before sunrise or after sunset. During full daylight, the sky is too bright for the plume to stand out, while in complete darkness there is no sunlight available to illuminate the exhaust.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/en/article/what-is-the-twilight-phenomenon-spacex-rocket/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Why Do Some Rocket Launches Look Like UFOs?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why Do Some Rocket Launches Look Like UFOs?October 8, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 8, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why plumes expand into spirals and luminous clouds

The dramatic shapes are a natural consequence of physics in the upper atmosphere.

At sea level, atmospheric pressure confines a rocket's exhaust into a relatively narrow plume. At orbital altitudes, where the air is extremely thin, the same gases expand rapidly in all directions, producing enormous translucent clouds hundreds of kilometres across from the observer's perspective. This is why photographs sometimes resemble giant jellyfish, nebulae or glowing discs rather than ordinary exhaust trails.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap260319.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apod.nasa.gov">[Astronomy Picture of the Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apod.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy Picture of the DayAPOD: 2026 March 19 – Launch Plume: SpaceX Jellyfish...</span></span></span>

The spiral pattern usually appears during operations involving an upper stage rather than the main ascent. [Common causes]({{ 'common-causes/' | relative_url }}) include:

* Rotation of the upper stage while stabilising its orientation.
* Controlled venting or dumping of excess propellant.
* Small attitude-control thrusters releasing gas while the stage spins.

As the stage rotates, the escaping gas traces out a corkscrew. Because the exhaust continues expanding while reflecting sunlight, observers see an ever-growing luminous spiral rather than a narrow helix. The pattern often remains visible for several minutes before fading as the particles disperse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sky.com/story/glowing-spiral-appears-in-night-sky-as-met-office-reveals-likely-cause-13335297" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sky.com">[sky.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sky.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Glowing spiral appears in night sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky NewsGlowing spiral appears in night sky - as Met Office reveals likely cause &#124; Science, Climate &amp; Tech News &#124; Sky NewsMarch 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2025</span></span></span>

Upper-level winds can further stretch or distort the plume, producing shapes that appear to pulse, blossom or change direction even though the underlying rocket follows a predictable trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

## Why these displays are mistaken for UFOs

Rocket twilight phenomena combine several visual cues that humans naturally associate with something extraordinary.

First, the illuminated cloud can appear enormous because observers have almost no depth references against a dark sky. A distant plume hundreds of kilometres away may seem like a nearby object spanning a significant part of the horizon.

Second, the visible glow often appears to hover or drift slowly. Although the rocket is travelling at several kilometres per second, the vast viewing distance compresses its apparent motion, making it seem almost stationary.

Third, the brightest part of the display is frequently not the rocket itself but the expanding exhaust. Witnesses therefore search unsuccessfully for a solid craft producing the light, reinforcing the impression that they are observing something unknown rather than conventional spaceflight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/en/article/what-is-the-twilight-phenomenon-spacex-rocket/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Why Do Some Rocket Launches Look Like UFOs?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why Do Some Rocket Launches Look Like UFOs?October 8, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 8, 2018</span></span></span>

## Clues that separate rocket effects from solid craft

Several characteristics consistently distinguish twilight rocket plumes from reports of structured flying objects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Timing:** They usually occur shortly after sunset or before sunrise, matching launch windows that produce twilight illumination.
* **Expansion:** The glowing feature steadily grows larger as the exhaust disperses rather than maintaining a fixed size.
* **Diffuse appearance:** Edges are soft and cloud-like instead of sharply defined.
* **Colour changes:** Blue, white, pink and orange hues often develop as sunlight scatters through expanding particles.
* **Predictable duration:** Most displays evolve over several minutes before gradually fading.
* **Launch correlation:** Public launch schedules and orbital tracking frequently match the time and direction of the sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

## Why rocket spirals matter in UFO investigations

Rocket twilight phenomena illustrate how an event can appear astonishing while remaining entirely consistent with established aerospace physics. They also show why eyewitness sincerity is not the same as identifying the underlying cause. A witness may accurately describe an immense glowing spiral or expanding cloud while naturally concluding that it represents an unknown craft because the rocket itself is invisible.

Within the broader history of UFO reports, these displays are among the strongest examples of ordinary technology producing genuinely unfamiliar appearances. Their recurrence across multiple countries and launch providers, together with their close correspondence to documented launch times and the known behaviour of high-altitude exhaust, makes them one of the clearest mechanisms by which spectacular but conventional events become enduring UFO stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>
